## Why Antigua Guatemala Attracts UK Buyers

The reasons British buyers choose Antigua Guatemala are straightforward. Property prices here are a fraction of what you would pay in the UK. A three-bedroom colonial home in a central Antigua neighbourhood can cost between £120,000 and £250,000. A comparable property in London or the Home Counties would cost five to ten times that amount.

The climate is another major draw. Antigua sits at 1,500 metres above sea level, giving it a year-round spring-like climate. Average daytime temperatures range from 18°C to 25°C. No central heating bills, no damp winters, no grey skies for months at a time.

Then there is the lifestyle. Antigua is a UNESCO World Heritage city with cobblestone streets, colonial architecture, and a thriving expat community. You get walkability, safety, and a social scene that includes everything from Spanish language schools to fine dining and live music.

## The GBP Advantage in Antigua's Real Estate Market

British buyers enjoy a significant currency advantage when purchasing property in Antigua Guatemala. The Guatemalan quetzal (GTQ) is pegged loosely to the US dollar, and the pound sterling has maintained strong purchasing power against both currencies.

As of September 2026, £1 buys approximately 10.2 GTQ. This means a property priced at 2,000,000 GTQ costs roughly £196,000. Two years ago, the same property would have cost closer to £220,000. The pound's strength against both the dollar and the quetzal has made Antigua property more affordable for UK buyers than at any point in recent memory.

This currency advantage also applies to ongoing costs. Monthly expenses for a couple living in Antigua — rent, utilities, food, and healthcare — typically run between £800 and £1,400 per month, depending on lifestyle. That is less than the average UK mortgage payment alone.

## Visa Pathways for British Citizens

British citizens can enter Guatemala visa-free and stay for up to 90 days. For longer stays, you have several options.

### Temporary Residency

The most common route for property buyers is temporary residency. You qualify by demonstrating a monthly income of at least $1,000 (approximately £770) from a pension, investments, or remote work. The application process takes three to six months and requires a Guatemalan lawyer to handle the paperwork. Once granted, temporary residency is valid for one or two years and is renewable.

### Digital Nomad Visa

Guatemala's digital nomad visa allows remote workers to stay for up to two years. You need to prove remote employment or freelance income of at least $500 per month. This visa is ideal for UK buyers who want to spend extended periods in Antigua before committing to full-time residency.

### Permanent Residency Through Investment

Buying property in Guatemala does not automatically grant residency, but it strengthens a residency application significantly. A property investment of $50,000 or more demonstrates your commitment to the country and makes approval more likely. For larger investments — typically $100,000 or more — you may qualify for a faster-track residency process.

## The Buying Process for UK Buyers

The property buying process in Guatemala is different from the UK. Here is what to expect.

### Finding a Property

Most UK buyers start their search online. Our [property listings](/properties/) include detailed descriptions, photos, and pricing in both USD and GTQ. Once you identify potential properties, a video tour or in-person visit is essential.

### Making an Offer

Once you find a property, your agent submits a formal offer to the seller. Negotiation is common, and offers typically include a proposed closing timeline. Expect to pay a reservation deposit of 5% to 10% once the offer is accepted.

### Due Diligence

Guatemala requires a thorough due diligence process. Your lawyer checks the property title, confirms there are no outstanding liens or debts, and verifies that all taxes are paid. This process typically takes two to four weeks.

### The Closing Process

Closing in Guatemala involves signing the final deed (escritura pública) before a notary. The notary registers the transfer with the property registry (Registro de la Propiedad). Closing costs typically range from 3% to 7% of the purchase price, including notary fees, registration fees, and transfer taxes.

### Legal Representation

Hiring a Guatemalan lawyer is not optional — it is required. Your lawyer handles the due diligence, drafts the purchase agreement, and ensures the transaction complies with Guatemalan law. Legal fees typically range from 1% to 2% of the property value. ## Best Neighbourhoods for British Buyers

Different areas of Antigua suit different lifestyles. Here are the most popular neighbourhoods among UK buyers.

### Centro Histórico

The historic centre is ideal for buyers who want to be in the heart of the action. Properties here are colonial-era homes with interior courtyards, high ceilings, and traditional architecture. Expect to pay a premium for location — a restored colonial home in Centro starts around £180,000.

### San Pedro

Just east of the centre, San Pedro is a quieter residential neighbourhood with a mix of colonial homes and modern construction. It is walkable to the main square and popular with families. Prices are more moderate than Centro, with good properties available from £100,000.

### Ciudad Vieja

Ten minutes south of Antigua, Ciudad Vieja offers larger lots and newer construction at lower prices. This area appeals to UK buyers who want more space and a quieter setting. A modern three-bedroom home in Ciudad Vieja can cost between £80,000 and £150,000. See our [Ciudad Vieja living guide](/blog/ciudad-vieja-living-guide/) for a deeper look.

### El Hato

Perched on the hillside above Antigua, El Hato offers panoramic volcano views and cooler temperatures. Properties here range from modern villas to rustic fincas. Prices vary widely, from £70,000 for a fixer-upper to £300,000 for a high-end villa. Read our [El Hato living guide](/blog/el-hato-living-guide-antigua/) for more details.

## UK Buyer Tax Considerations

British buyers should understand the tax implications before purchasing property in Guatemala.

### Property Taxes

Guatemala property taxes are low compared to the UK. The annual property tax (IUSI) ranges from 0.2% to 0.6% of the registered property value, depending on the municipality. For a £150,000 property, expect to pay roughly £300 to £900 per year in property tax.

### UK Tax Obligations

As a UK resident, you remain liable for UK taxes on worldwide income. Rental income from a Guatemalan property must be declared to HMRC. However, a double taxation agreement between the UK and Guatemala means you will not pay tax twice. You receive credit for Guatemalan taxes paid when filing your UK return.

### Capital Gains

If you sell a Guatemalan property as a non-resident, capital gains are taxed in Guatemala at 10% of the gain. UK capital gains tax may also apply depending on your residency status and how long you have owned the property.

## Common Pitfalls for UK Buyers

British buyers face a few unique challenges when buying in Antigua. Here is what to watch for.

**Currency fluctuation.** While the pound is currently strong, exchange rates change. Consider wiring funds in stages rather than a single transfer to average out the rate. A forward contract with a currency specialist can lock in a favourable rate for up to 12 months.

**Language barrier.** While many real estate agents and lawyers in Antigua speak English, legal documents are in Spanish. Always use a bilingual lawyer and request translated copies of all documents.

**Rushing the process.** Guatemala's property market moves at a different pace than the UK. Due diligence, title searches, and registration take time. Budget three to six months from offer to closing.

**Not visiting first.** While video tours are useful, buying sight unseen carries risk. Visit Antigua, walk the neighbourhoods, and see properties in person before committing.
