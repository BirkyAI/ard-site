You found a property listing in Antigua Guatemala that looks perfect online. The photos are stunning, the price seems right, and the location checks all your boxes. Now comes the real test: the property viewing.

Walking through a home in person reveals things no listing photo can capture. Colonial homes have unique quirks. Modern builds have their own considerations. And the neighborhood dynamics matter just as much as the house itself.

This checklist covers what to look for, what questions to ask, and what red flags to watch for when viewing properties in Antigua Guatemala.

## Before the Viewing: Do Your Homework

### Check the Neighborhood at Different Times

A quiet street at 10 AM might be a noisy thoroughfare during rush hour or a party hub on weekends. Visit the area at different times before you commit. Walk the street in the evening. Stop by on a Saturday afternoon.

Key questions for neighborhood evaluation:
- Is there street parking available, and is it safe overnight?
- How close are the nearest tienda, market, and pharmacy?
- What is the noise level like from nearby bars, churches, or schools?
- Are there street dogs that bark through the night?
- How far is the nearest ATM, grocery store, and hospital?

Antigua's neighborhoods vary dramatically. The historic center offers walkability but can be noisy. Ciudad Vieja is quieter with more space. El Hato offers mountain views and fresh air but requires a vehicle. Our [neighborhood guides](/neighborhoods/) break down each area in detail.

### Review the Property Documents

Ask your real estate agent for the following before the viewing:

- Property title (escritura) to verify ownership
- Current tax receipts (boletas de arbitrios)
- Water and electric utility account numbers
- HOA or condominium fees if applicable
- Any existing construction permits or renovation records

For colonial homes, also ask about:
- Heritage designation status (the Instituto de Antropologia e Historia has rules about modifications)
- Previous restoration work and who did it
- Any structural engineering reports

## Inside the Property: What to Inspect

### Walls and Ceilings

Colonial homes in Antigua were built with adobe, calicanto, or a combination of materials. Each has different maintenance needs.

**Look for:**
- Cracks in walls -- hairline cracks are normal in older homes, but wide or diagonal cracks could indicate foundation issues
- Water stains on ceilings or upper walls -- signs of past or current roof leaks
- Bubbling or peeling paint -- often indicates humidity problems
- Patched areas -- ask what was behind the patch

**Run your hand along the walls.** If they feel damp, especially during the dry season, you will have humidity issues during the rainy months (May through October).

### Roof Condition

The roof is the most critical and expensive component of any Antigua property. A full roof replacement on a colonial home can cost $15,000 to $30,000 USD.

**What to check:**
- Original clay tile (teja) versus replacement roofing
- Signs of sagging or uneven rooflines
- Interior ceiling stains near walls -- indicate roof leaks
- Attic or crawl space if accessible -- look for daylight coming through
- Recent roof work receipts -- a new roof in the last 5 years is a big plus

### Plumbing and Water Systems

Water is a major consideration in Antigua. Some areas have municipal water only a few hours per day, while others have 24-hour service.

**Essential checks:**
- Turn on ALL faucets -- check water pressure in the kitchen, each bathroom, and outdoor spigots
- Flush every toilet and listen for running water afterward
- Check for water tank (tinaco) on the roof or in the yard
- Ask about cistern capacity and water delivery frequency
- Look under sinks for signs of leaks or corrosion on pipes
- Confirm the hot water system -- is it a tank, tankless, or solar?
- Check the water filtration system if one is installed

In our rental at [Finca El Tambor in El Hato](/properties/rental-casa-contenta-el-hato/), properties have their own water sources and storage. In the historic center, many homes rely on municipal supply plus cisterns. Understanding the water setup is critical for every property.

### Electrical System

Antigua's older homes often have outdated electrical systems that may not support modern appliances or air conditioning.

**What to inspect:**
- Age of the electrical panel -- is it modern breaker panel or old fuse box?
- Number of outlets in each room -- older homes are notoriously short on outlets
- Presence of GFCI outlets in kitchens and bathrooms
- Grounded (three-prong) outlets versus ungrounded
- Air conditioning readiness -- does the panel have capacity for mini-splits?
- Light switches that feel loose or spark -- signs of faulty wiring

Budget for electrical upgrades if the property has original wiring. A full rewire for a typical colonial home costs $3,000 to $8,000 depending on size.

### Windows and Doors

Colonial windows and doors are beautiful but often drafty. Modern buyers frequently replace or supplement them.

**Checklist:**
- Do windows open and close properly?
- Are there security bars (enrejado) on windows and doors?
- Is there glass in the windows, or just wooden shutters?
- Check for gaps around window frames -- these let in dust, rain, and insects
- Do exterior doors have deadbolts or robust locks?
- Are there mosquito screens on operable windows?

### Flooring

Antigua properties feature everything from original clay tile (baldosas de barro) to modern porcelain, hardwood, or polished concrete.

**What to look for:**
- Condition of clay tiles -- are they cracked, chipped, or worn?
- Is the flooring level, or does it slope? Some slope is normal in very old homes
- In bathrooms, check for soft spots around the shower and toilet (water damage)
- Ask when the flooring was last replaced or refinished

## Outdoor Spaces: Gardens, Patios, and Terraces

Outdoor living is a defining feature of Antigua properties. The patio or garden can make or break your daily experience.

**Inspect:**
- Drainage in the patio during rain -- does water pool anywhere?
- Condition of garden walls and fences
- Existing plants and trees -- are they well-maintained or overgrown?
- Privacy from neighboring properties
- Sun exposure throughout the day
- Outdoor water access and irrigation systems
- Parking -- is there a garage, carport, or designated street parking?

If you are considering [pre-construction homes in Antigua Guatemala](/blog/pre-construction-homes-antigua-guatemala/), you have more control over outdoor space design. Existing properties require a more careful eye.

## Red Flags: When to Walk Away

Some issues are manageable with the right budget. Others should make you reconsider.

### Dealbreakers

- **Active structural cracks** -- wide, growing, or accompanied by shifted door frames
- **Chronic roof leaks** -- multiple stains on different ceilings suggest systemic failure
- **Sewage smell** -- indicates broken septic or municipal connection issues
- **Visible mold in multiple rooms** -- a health risk, not just cosmetic
- **No legal access** -- the property must have a clear legal path (servidumbre) to the street
- **Unresolved inheritance** -- properties with multiple heirs can take years to clear title

### Negotiable Issues

- **Outdated kitchen and bathrooms** -- budget $10,000-$25,000 for a full renovation
- **Old electrical or plumbing** -- factor in $5,000-$10,000 for upgrades
- **Single-pane windows** -- add $2,000-$5,000 for modern windows with better insulation
- **Paint and cosmetic updates** -- the cheapest fix, budget $2,000-$5,000

For a realistic picture of renovation costs, check our guide on [home renovation costs in Antigua Guatemala](/blog/home-renovation-costs-antigua-guatemala/).

## Questions to Ask the Seller or Agent

Bring these questions to every viewing:

1. Why is the seller moving?
2. How long has the property been on the market?
3. Have there been any offers that fell through -- and why?
4. What renovations or repairs have been done in the last 5 years?
5. Are there any known issues with the property?
6. What are the monthly utility costs (water, electric, garbage)?
7. What is the property tax bill (IUSI)?
8. Are the utilities registered in the seller's name, and can they be transferred?
9. Is there any pending maintenance or HOA special assessment?
10. What is the neighborhood like for short-term or long-term rentals?

The last question is important if you are considering using the property as an [investment rental](/properties/). Different neighborhoods and property types have different rental dynamics.
