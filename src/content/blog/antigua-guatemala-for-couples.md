More couples are discovering Antigua Guatemala as a place to build a life together. Not just retirees, though plenty of empty nesters are here too, but remote work duos, semi-retired partners, and couples in their 30s and 40s looking for a lower cost of living, better climate, and a slower pace without sacrificing quality of life.

This guide covers what couples need to know before making the move: housing options that work for two, realistic monthly costs, how to build a social circle, and the practical logistics of settling in together.

## Why Couples Are Choosing Antigua

The decision to relocate as a couple comes with different considerations than moving solo. Both partners need to feel good about the choice. Antigua checks the boxes that matter most for pairs.

**Cost of living stretches further for two.** A couple can live well in Antigua on $1,800 to $2,500 per month, rent, utilities, groceries, dining out, and activities included. That same budget in most US or European cities covers rent alone. The savings open up options: one partner can work less, both can travel more, or you simply enjoy a higher standard of living.

**The lifestyle suits both active and quiet days.** Want to hike a volcano before breakfast? Done. Prefer to spend the afternoon reading on a rooftop terrace? Equally valid. Antigua accommodates both energies without forcing a choice. Couples who have different ideas of a great day both get what they want.

**Safety and walkability matter for pairs.** Antigua's compact historic centre is walkable end to end in about twenty minutes. Couples can stroll to dinner, coffee, the market, and back without needing a car. The city is consistently ranked as one of the safest in Central America, which matters when both partners want to explore independently during the day.

**The expat community is welcoming to couples.** Unlike some destinations where the social scene revolves around solo backpackers or retirees, Antigua's expat community includes a healthy mix of couples at every stage, newly arrived, long-settled, working, retired. Finding your people takes weeks, not months.

## Best Neighbourhoods for Couples

Different areas of Antigua suit different couple dynamics. Here is how the main neighbourhoods compare.

### Centro Histórico. For Walkability and Vibe

The historic centre is ideal for couples who want to step out their front door and be in the middle of everything. Restaurants, cafes, galleries, and the central plaza are all within a five-minute walk.

**Typical rental:** A one-bedroom colonial apartment in the centre runs $500 to $800 per month. A two-bedroom with a courtyard or rooftop terrace is $800 to $1,200.

**Best for:** Couples who value walkability, enjoy being near the social scene, and want the full colonial experience. The trade-off is noise, weekends can be lively near the plaza.

### San Pedro. Quieter, More Residential

Just east of the centre, San Pedro offers a more relaxed pace while still being walkable to town. The streets are quieter, the sidewalks wider, and the properties often include gardens or outdoor space.

**Typical rental:** $400 to $700 per month for a one or two-bedroom home. Many properties in San Pedro include small gardens where couples can grow plants or sit outside in the afternoon sun.

**Best for:** Couples who want peace and quiet without being isolated. San Pedro is close enough to walk to the centre in ten to fifteen minutes but far enough that you escape the tourist crowds.

### Jocotenango. Community Feel Near the Arch

Jocotenango, clustered around the iconic Santa Catalina arch, has its own micro-community with cafes, a Saturday market, and a neighbourhood feel. It is a ten-minute walk from the main plaza.

**Typical rental:** $500 to $900 per month. The area around the arch is popular with expats, so availability is good and landlords are accustomed to foreign tenants.

**Best for:** Couples who want to be part of a built-in community. Jocotenango's expat density means you will meet neighbours quickly. Wednesday night trivia at a local bar is a real thing here.

### Ciudad Vieja. Space and Value

For couples who want a house with a garden, space to spread out, and lower rent, Ciudad Vieja is worth the trade-off. It is a ten-minute tuk-tuk ride from central Antigua.

**Typical rental:** $350 to $600 per month for a two or three-bedroom home with outdoor space. You get significantly more square footage for your money.

**Best for:** Couples with a car or scooter who prioritise space over walkability. Ideal for partners who work from home and appreciate having a dedicated office room. See our [Ciudad Vieja living guide](/blog/ciudad-vieja-living-guide/) for a detailed look at the area.

## Housing Options for Couples

### Colonial Apartment

The most common setup for couples in Antigua is a colonial apartment, typically the upper floor of a restored colonial house, with high ceilings, a private entrance, and often a shared courtyard or rooftop terrace.

**Pros:** Character, location, natural light. Many come partially furnished.
**Cons:** Noise from the street or neighbouring units. Colonial construction means thin walls and sometimes limited hot water.

### House with Garden

A standalone house with outdoor space is achievable outside the historic centre. San Pedro, Jocotenango, and Ciudad Vieja all have houses with small gardens and off-street parking.

**Pros:** Privacy, space, ability to host friends.
**Cons:** Higher rent, more maintenance, further from the centre.

### Entire Colonial Home

For couples with a larger budget, renting or buying a full colonial house is transformative. These properties typically feature interior courtyards, multiple rooms, rooftop terraces, and volcanic views. Our [property listings](/properties/) include several restored colonial homes available for purchase starting around $120,000.

**Pros:** Unbeatable character, complete privacy, investment potential.
**Cons:** Significant financial commitment, ongoing maintenance of an older property.

## Lifestyle as a Couple in Antigua

### What You Will Spend Your Days Doing

Antigua offers couples a rhythm that is hard to replicate elsewhere. A typical week might include:

- **Morning coffee** at a cafe with volcano views. Café Bohemia or La Cosecha are couple favourites
- **Spanish class** together, most language schools offer partner discounts for couples enrolling together
- **Weekend hikes** up Volcán Pacaya or Acatenango, guided tours run $40 to $80 per person
- **Cooking classes** featuring Guatemalan cuisine, several schools in town offer couple-friendly sessions
- **Wine nights** at rooftop bars. El Muro and Sky Bar are popular for sunset drinks
- **Day trips** to Lake Atitlán, coffee plantations, or the Pacific coast on long weekends

### Building a Social Circle

The fastest way for couples to meet people in Antigua:

1. **Join Impact Hub**, the coworking space hosts weekly events where locals and expats mix. A dual membership costs roughly $150/month for two.
2. **Attend language exchanges**. Wednesday and Friday evenings at several cafes around town. Free, social, and full of new arrivals.
3. **Take a class together**, salsa dancing, pottery, or Spanish lessons. You meet people with shared interests immediately.
4. **Use the Facebook groups**. "Expats in Antigua Guatemala" and "Digital Nomads in Antigua" post weekly events. Couples-specific meetups happen regularly.

## Practical Considerations for Couples

### Both Partners Working Remotely

If both of you work from home, internet reliability matters. Antigua has strong fibre coverage in the centre, Jocotenango, and San Pedro. Speeds of 100 to 200 Mbps are standard. Plan for a dedicated workspace, two partners in a one-bedroom apartment can work fine if you set up separate zones. For a deeper look at connectivity, read our [internet guide for expats](/blog/internet-wifi-expats-antigua-guatemala/).

### Healthcare for Two

Antigua has several private clinics with English-speaking doctors. A consultation costs $25 to $50. Private health insurance for a couple runs $100 to $250 per month depending on coverage level. See our [healthcare guide](/blog/antigua-guatemala-healthcare-expats/) for details.

### Getting Around

Most couples get by without a car in the first year. Tuk-tuks cost $1 to $3 per ride within Antigua. For longer trips, Uber and shared shuttles are available. If you decide to buy, a scooter or small car is sufficient for Antigua's compact geography. Our guide on [getting around without a car](/blog/getting-around-antigua-without-car/) covers the options.

### Visas and Residency

Couples who stay longer than 90 days need a visa plan. The most common approach is the 90-day tourist visa with a renewal trip to El Salvador or Belize every three months. For longer stays, Guatemala offers temporary residency for those with a monthly income of $1,000 or more (per applicant) and a growing digital nomad visa option. Read our [residency through real estate guide](/blog/guatemala-residency-through-real-estate/) and [digital nomad visa guide](/blog/guatemala-digital-nomad-visa/) for the full breakdown.

## Is Antigua Right for You as a Couple?

Antigua Guatemala works best for couples who:

- Want a slower pace without sacrificing amenities
- Value walkability and colonial character over beach access
- Are comfortable with Spanish or willing to learn
- Have at least one remote income source
- Enjoy exploring together and independently

It is less ideal for couples who need consistent beach weather, prefer big-city nightlife, or want ultra-fast connectivity for latency-sensitive work.
