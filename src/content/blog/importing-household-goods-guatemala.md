Importing household goods to Guatemala is one of the biggest logistical hurdles when relocating, but understanding the process upfront saves you time, money, and frustration. Whether you are shipping a full container or bringing essentials in suitcases, this guide covers what you need to know.

## Guatemala Customs Basics for Personal Belongings

Guatemala's customs authority, the SAT (Superintendencia de Administración Tributaria), allows incoming residents to import personal household goods with a duty exemption. The key requirement is that you hold a valid residency visa (temporary or permanent) before your shipment arrives.

Without residency status, your goods are treated as commercial imports and face full tariffs, which range from 0% to 15% depending on the item category, plus a 12% VAT (IVA). With residency, you get a one-time exemption on used personal belongings.

The exemption covers furniture, appliances, clothing, books, kitchenware, linens, and personal electronics. It does not cover vehicles, alcohol, tobacco, or items that appear brand new and in commercial quantities.

## What You Can and Cannot Bring

Guatemala is relatively relaxed compared to some countries, but there are clear restrictions.

**Allowed without special permits:**
- Furniture and home decor
- Kitchen appliances and cookware
- Clothing and personal items
- Books, artwork, and musical instruments
- Computers, tablets, and personal electronics
- Tools for personal use

**Restricted or prohibited:**
- Firearms and ammunition (require separate permits from the Ministry of Defense)
- Plants and seeds (require phytosanitary certificates)
- Medications beyond personal use (prescription required)
- Alcohol in quantities that suggest resale
- Used tires
- Any items violating intellectual property rights

**Pro tip:** If you are bringing high-value electronics or art, photograph everything and keep purchase receipts. Customs may ask for proof that items are personal use, not commercial inventory.

## Shipping Options to Guatemala

You have three main options for getting your belongings to Antigua Guatemala.

**Full container (FCL):** A 20-foot container from the US to Puerto Quetzal typically costs $2,500 to $4,500 depending on your origin city. Transit time is 10 to 21 days. This is the best option if you are shipping an entire household.

**Less than container (LCL):** If you do not fill a container, your goods share space with other shipments. Costs run $150 to $300 per cubic meter, but transit times are longer (3 to 6 weeks) because of consolidation and deconsolidation stops.

**Air freight:** Fastest but most expensive. Expect $4 to $8 per kilogram. Only practical for essential items you need immediately. Transit is 3 to 7 days door to door.

**Recommended approach:** Many expats shipping to Antigua Guatemala use a combination. Ship the bulk by sea container, then carry essential items (documents, medications, a few weeks of clothing) in checked luggage on your flight.

## Required Documents for Customs Clearance

Prepare these documents before your shipment leaves. Missing paperwork is the number one cause of delays at Guatemalan customs.

1. **Passport** with valid Guatemalan visa or residency card
2. **Bill of Lading** (for sea shipments) or Air Waybill
3. **Detailed inventory** in Spanish, itemizing every box with contents and estimated value
4. **Proof of residence** in Guatemala (rental contract or property deed)
5. **Origin certificate** from your previous country (utility bill or bank statement showing your address)
6. **Cedula** or DPI (Guatemalan national ID) if you already have one

The inventory must be in Spanish and list each item individually. Vague entries like "box of household items" will trigger a physical inspection, which adds days to the process and costs extra in storage fees.

## Cost Expectations and Budgeting

Beyond shipping costs, budget for these additional expenses:

- **Customs broker (agente aduanal):** $300 to $600 for handling the clearance process. Highly recommended, as navigating SAT offices without local knowledge is extremely difficult.
- **Port handling fees:** $100 to $250 at Puerto Quetzal
- **Inland transport:** $200 to $500 from Puerto Quetzal to Antigua Guatemala (about 2.5 hours by truck)
- **Storage fees:** $20 to $40 per day if your goods sit at the port while paperwork is sorted
- **Insurance:** 2% to 3% of declared shipment value for transit coverage

For a typical household shipment (20-foot container from the US), expect a total landed cost of $3,500 to $6,000 including all fees, customs broker, and inland delivery.

## Tips for a Smooth Import Process

**Hire a local customs broker.** This is the single best investment you can make. A good broker in Guatemala City or Puerto Quetzal handles the paperwork, communicates with SAT, and prevents costly mistakes. Ask your real estate agent for a referral.

**Time your shipment with your visa.** Your residency must be approved before the goods arrive. If the shipment lands before your visa is processed, it sits in port storage at $20 to $40 per day. Coordinate carefully.

**Ship during dry season.** Guatemala's rainy season (May through October) can cause delays in inland transport, especially on mountain roads to Antigua. If possible, schedule delivery for November through April.

**Pack for the journey.** Guatemala's roads are rough, especially the final stretch into Antigua. Use extra padding on fragile items, and avoid shipping anything that cannot handle vibration and humidity.

**Keep originals, ship copies.** Carry all important documents (passport, visa, property papers, insurance) in your personal luggage. Ship only copies with your goods.

## Settling In After Your Shipment Arrives

Once your container clears customs and arrives in Antigua, the unpacking begins. Most expats find that they need fewer belongings than they expected. The colonial homes in Antigua have smaller rooms than typical North American houses, so large furniture pieces sometimes do not fit through doorways or up narrow staircases.

Consider selling or donating oversized items before you ship, and plan to buy some furniture locally. Guatemala has skilled carpenters and furniture makers who can build custom pieces at a fraction of US prices.
