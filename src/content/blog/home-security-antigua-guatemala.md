Antigua Guatemala is widely considered one of the safest cities in Central America for expats and foreign residents. The colonial city has a strong sense of community, visible police presence in tourist areas, and relatively low violent crime rates. However, like any popular destination, petty theft and property crime do occur. Taking sensible home security measures is part of responsible expat living. This guide covers everything you need to know about keeping your home and family safe in Antigua Guatemala.

## Security in Antigua Guatemala: The Big Picture

Antigua Guatemala benefits from being a compact, walkable city with a tight-knit expat community. The main tourist areas around the Central Park, Arco de Santa Catalina, and the cobblestone streets of the historic center are well patrolled by both municipal police and tourist police. Many neighborhoods have active WhatsApp groups where residents share security updates and look out for each other.

That said, expats should take standard precautions. Opportunistic theft, such as items taken from unlocked vehicles or open ground-floor windows, is the most common issue. Home burglaries are rare but not unheard of, particularly in properties that appear uninhabited for long periods or have obvious security gaps. The good news is that most security problems are preventable with basic measures.

## Essential Home Security Measures

### Secure Doors and Windows

The most effective security upgrade for any home in Antigua Guatemala is reinforced entry points. Many colonial homes have original wooden doors that look beautiful but may not provide adequate security. Adding a heavy-duty deadbolt, security bar, or reinforced strike plate transforms an ornamental door into a real barrier.

Ground-floor windows should have locking mechanisms and, ideally, security bars or grilles. In the historic center, visible street-facing bars must comply with conservation guidelines, but rear windows and courtyard-facing windows can be secured more freely. Sliding glass doors, common in newer homes and apartments, benefit from a simple dowel or track lock placed in the frame.

### Alarm Systems

Professional alarm systems are widely available in Antigua Guatemala. Several local companies offer monitored systems that alert a security response team if triggered. Monthly monitoring fees range from Q200 to Q500 (approximately $26 to $65 USD), depending on the level of service and number of sensors.

A basic system includes door and window sensors, motion detectors, and a control panel. More advanced options include glass-break detectors, outdoor motion sensors with cameras, and smartphone integration that lets you arm, disarm, and monitor your system remotely. Most companies offer English-language support and installation within a few days.

### Security Cameras

Modern security cameras are affordable and easy to install. Wi-Fi cameras from brands like Ring, Eufy, and Google Nest work well in Antigua Guatemala, provided your home has reliable internet. These cameras offer motion alerts, night vision, two-way audio, and cloud storage.

Key placement areas include the main entrance, driveway or parking area, and any ground-floor access points. A visible camera at the front door is often enough to deter casual thieves. For larger properties, consider a multi-camera system with a dedicated network video recorder.

## Security Guards and Guarded Communities

Many expats in Antigua Guatemala choose to live in gated communities or buildings with a 24-hour guard service. In neighborhoods like El Hato, San Miguel Duenas, and the newer developments on the outskirts of town, security guards are standard. A guard typically checks vehicles entering the community, monitors security cameras, and patrols the area at night.

For standalone homes, hiring a private security guard is an option. Monthly costs range from Q3,500 to Q5,500 ($460 to $720 USD) for 12-hour shifts. Most expats find that joining a community with shared guard services is more cost-effective than individual hiring.

## Smart Locks and Keyless Entry

Keyless entry systems are growing in popularity among Antigua Guatemala expats, especially for rental properties and vacation homes. Smart locks allow you to grant access without physical keys, set temporary codes for guests and cleaners, and monitor entry logs from your phone.

Installation is straightforward for most modern doors. Popular options include August, Schlage Encode, and Yale brands, all available through online retailers that ship to Guatemala. Battery life typically lasts six months to a year, and low-battery alerts prevent lockouts.

## What About Insurance?

Home and contents insurance is recommended for all expats in Antigua Guatemala. Standard policies cover theft, fire, natural disasters, and liability. Most insurers require proof of basic security measures (deadbolts, window locks, alarm system) before issuing a policy.

## Practical Daily Habits

Beyond hardware and systems, simple habits make a big difference. Always lock doors and windows when leaving, even for a short errand. Do not leave valuables visible in parked cars. Get to know your neighbors, especially other expats on your street. Keep exterior lights on at night. And if you are traveling, ask a neighbor or property manager to check on your home periodically.
