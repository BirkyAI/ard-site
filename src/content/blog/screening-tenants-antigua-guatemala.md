In Guatemala there is no credit score to pull and no national register of renters, so checking a tenant is manual work. You verify identity, income and references yourself, in writing, before you hand over the keys. Done properly, it is the single step that decides whether a rental quietly pays for itself or turns into months of chasing rent.

A large part of the rental market here runs through WhatsApp, cash and goodwill, which is exactly why a written record matters more rather than less.

## Why is screening a tenant different in Guatemala?

There is no local equivalent of a FICO score and no central tenancy database to query. A landlord cannot buy a report and read a history of missed payments. What exists instead is paperwork you gather and calls you make.

Residential leases in Guatemala are governed by the Código Civil (Decreto Ley 106), so a landlord leans on documents, references and a signed contract rather than a credit score. The point of screening is not paperwork for its own sake: it is knowing that the person moving in can pay, that someone stands behind them if they cannot, and that you can prove what was agreed.

## What documents should you ask a prospective tenant for?

Ask everyone for the same set, so no one feels singled out, and put the request in writing. A short, professional list also tells a serious tenant that you run things properly.

| What to ask for | Why it matters | What to check |
| Valid ID (DPI, or passport for a foreign tenant) | Confirms who is actually signing | Name matches the lease and the bank details |
| Proof of income | Shows the rent is affordable | Payslip, contract, or recent bank statements if self-employed |
| Two previous landlord references | The closest thing to a rental history here | Call them, do not just collect numbers |
| Guarantor details, if one is used | Gives you a second route to recover losses | That the fiador understands and accepts the role |
| A written lease signed by both sides | Sets rent, term, deposit and repairs | Every blank filled in before signing |
| Deposit and inventory agreed in writing | Prevents arguments at the end | What the deposit covers is spelled out |

## How do guarantors work in Guatemala?

A fiador is a person who agrees, in the lease, to answer for the rent if the tenant does not pay. It is a common requirement when a tenant has no local rental history, and the strongest version is a fiador de propiedad, a guarantor who owns property in Guatemala that could be pursued. For a foreign tenant who has just arrived, a local fiador or a larger deposit is often the practical compromise. Either way, the guarantor's role has to be written into the lease and signed, because a verbal promise to stand behind a tenant is worth very little later.

## How much deposit is normal, and what does it cover?

Roughly one month's rent is the customary deposit here, normally held against unpaid rent or damage beyond ordinary wear. Say in the lease what the deposit covers, when it is returned and how a disagreement is settled, so a normal handback does not become a dispute. An inventory, ideally with photos, does most of that work for you. If the tenant brings furniture or pets, record the condition of the property at the start.

## How do you verify income without a credit report?

Ask for the document that fits the person in front of you. A salaried employee can produce a payslip, a work contract and a letter from an employer you can phone to confirm. Someone self-employed can show recent bank statements and, where relevant, a business registration. A foreign tenant who lives on a pension or a remote salary can show the deposits arriving month by month. Many owners here look for the rent to sit around a third of verified monthly income, though a strong fiador can offset a tighter ratio.

## When should you register the lease?

A private lease between two people works, but registering it at the Registro de la Propiedad is what makes it enforceable against third parties. That matters if you sell the property while the tenant is still inside. It is a notarial step, so it costs something and takes time, and it is usually reserved for longer terms and higher-value properties. If you plan to sell, [buying a rental with tenants](/blog/buying-rental-with-tenants-antigua-guatemala/) explains what a registered lease means for the buyer. The tax and registration duties on your side are set out in [landlord and tenant rights in Guatemala](/blog/landlord-tenant-rights-guatemala/).

## What are the warning signs to walk away from?

- Pressure to move in immediately, before any paperwork is done.
- Reluctance to sign anything written or to be named on a contract.
- Cash offered for several months up front with no receipt trail.
- References that are given but never reachable, or that turn out to be a friend.
- Vagueness about where the income comes from.
- A plan to re-let the property short term when your lease is long term.
- Refusal of an inventory or a walkthrough before moving in.

## How is screening different for a short-term rental?

For a nightly or weekly let, most verification happens on the booking platform: a verified identity, past reviews, and a deposit or pre-authorisation. A few rules cover the rest. Cap the occupancy, set quiet hours, take the ID of the person checking in, and put the house rules in writing before arrival. Short lets also carry their own local rules, covered in [short-term rental regulations](/blog/short-term-rental-regulations-antigua-guatemala-2026/).

## A simple screening checklist

Work through the same list every time, keep the documents somewhere safe, and note the date of each reference call.

- Identity verified and matched to the signer.
- Income confirmed by document and, where possible, by a phone call.
- Two previous landlord references actually contacted.
- Guarantor confirmed in writing, if one is used.
- Deposit, inventory and return conditions agreed before handover.
- Lease signed by all parties, with every blank filled in.
- Meter readings and keys logged on the day.
