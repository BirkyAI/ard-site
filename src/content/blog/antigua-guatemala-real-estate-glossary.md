# Antigua Guatemala Real Estate Glossary for Buyers

Every property purchase in Antigua Guatemala happens in Spanish. Your agent, notary and lawyer will hand you documents full of terms that never appear in a North American or European contract: folio real, finca, escritura, usufructo. Knowing them before you sign is the difference between a confident purchase and a month of expensive confusion.

This glossary explains the Spanish terms you will actually see when buying property in Antigua Guatemala, grouped by what they do. The definitions reflect the Guatemalan market and legal process as of 2026.

## The people and offices in a Guatemalan purchase

In Guatemala the buyer, seller and notary each have a defined role, and one person often wears two hats.

- **Notario (notary):** A licensed attorney who drafts the deed and certifies the sale. In Guatemala a notary is always a lawyer and carries legal liability for the transfer. Your notary may represent both sides, so many foreign buyers hire their own.
- **Abogado (lawyer):** Handles due diligence, title review and contracts. Hiring an attorney who does not work for the seller is standard advice for foreign buyers.
- **Agente inmobiliario (real estate agent):** Markets the property and coordinates viewings. Agents are not licensed by the state, so vetting matters.
- **Registro General de la Propiedad:** The national property registry where ownership is recorded. A sale is not legally complete until it is inscribed here.
- **Municipalidad:** The local government office that issues property tax records and construction permits.
- **Banco (bank):** Issues mortgages and can hold funds in escrow. Few banks lend to foreigners, so most foreign buyers pay cash or use seller financing.

## The documents you will sign

Guatemalan property paperwork is built around one registry record and one deed.

- **Escritura pública (public deed):** The formal deed of sale, executed before a notary. This is the document that transfers ownership.
- **Testimonio (certified copy):** A certified copy of the escritura, issued after it is registered. Your testimonio is your proof of ownership.
- **Primer testimonio:** The first certified copy issued to the buyer. Later copies are simply called testimonios.
- **Folio real:** The official registry record for one property, showing its chain of owners, liens and mortgages.
- **Finca:** The registry number of a registered rural or urban property. The finca number is how the registry identifies the land.
- **Matrícula fiscal:** The tax identification number the municipality assigns to a property.
- **Catastro:** The municipal survey office that records the physical boundaries and value of a property.
- **Plano (plat):** The surveyor's drawing of a property's boundaries and dimensions.

## Money, taxes and fees

- **Enganche (down payment):** The portion of the price paid up front to secure the purchase. A typical private sale asks for 10 to 30 percent.
- **Saldo (balance):** The remaining amount due at closing.
- **IVA (value-added tax):** A 12 percent tax charged on commercial property, goods and services. Residential resales between individuals are generally exempt.
- **Impuesto de timbres:** A stamp duty applied to certain contracts and documents.
- **IUSI:** The annual municipal property tax, typically a fraction of one percent of the registered value.
- **Arancel:** The registry and administrative fee paid to inscribe a transfer.
- **Honorarios notariales:** The notary's professional fee, often set as a percentage of the sale price.
- **Impuesto sobre la renta:** Capital gains tax, owed by the seller on profit from the sale.

## Land, size and permitted use

- **Vara:** An old Spanish unit of length, about 0.84 meters or 2.75 feet. Local listings still quote frontage in varas.
- **Manzana:** The standard unit for land area, about 1.72 acres or 0.7 hectares.
- **Caballería:** A larger colonial unit, about 110 acres.
- **Linderos:** The boundary lines of a property, described in the deed.
- **Uso de suelo:** The permitted use of a parcel, residential, commercial or mixed. Antigua's heritage rules can restrict what you build.
- **Propiedad horizontal:** The legal regime for condominiums, where each unit is owned separately and the common areas are shared.

## Ownership structures and rights

- **Usufructo (usufruct):** The right to use and profit from a property without owning it, often granted for life.
- **Fideicomiso (trust):** A legal trust that holds assets for a beneficiary. Useful for estate planning and for some foreign ownership structures.
- **Sociedad anónima:** A Guatemalan corporation. Some buyers hold property through one for liability or succession reasons.
- **Condominio:** Shared ownership of common areas in a building or gated community.
- **Gravamen:** A lien or encumbrance on a property, such as a mortgage or a legal claim.
- **Hipoteca:** A mortgage registered against a property.
- **Derecho de vía:** A right of way that lets others cross part of a property.

## How to use this glossary when you buy

A glossary is only useful if it changes what you do. When your notary sends the draft escritura, read it against the finca record at the Registro General de la Propiedad and confirm the linderos match the plano. Ask which gravámenes appear on the folio real. Confirm the uso de suelo allows what you plan to build. And if any term here shows up in a contract you have not seen before, stop and ask for a written explanation in your language before you sign.

Most confusion in a Guatemalan purchase comes from one word the buyer never asked about. A good agent and a notary who works for you will translate every document, and they expect you to ask.
