# Importing a Car to Guatemala: Costs and Process

Importing a car to Guatemala is legal, predictable, and cheaper on taxes than most expats expect, but the paperwork punishes anyone who improvises. If you are moving to Antigua Guatemala and want your own vehicle on the road, the import bill for a normal five-seat car lands at roughly 32 percent of what you paid for it, and the car clears customs in one to two weeks once your documents are in order.

## Can a foreigner import a car to Guatemala?

Yes. Guatemala places no nationality restriction on vehicle imports or registration. A tourist can bring a car in under a temporary import permit for the length of the stay, and once you hold residency you can import the vehicle permanently and register it in your own name.

The catch is that residency does not make the car duty free. The one-time "menaje de casa" exemption that lets new residents bring in furniture, appliances, and personal effects at zero tax explicitly excludes vehicles. You will pay full import tax on a car even if your container of household goods arrived tax free a month earlier.

## What does it cost to import a car to Guatemala?

Three taxes apply, and they are simpler than the rumors suggest. Customs duty (DAI) on cars is zero, value added tax (IVA) is 12 percent, and IPRIMA, the first-registration tax, is 20 percent for a car, SUV, or pickup that carries up to five people.

| Cost line | Rate or amount | What it covers |
|:--|:--|:--|
| DAI (import duty) | 0% | Guatemala's Central American tariff line on cars and motorcycles is zero |
| IVA (value added tax) | 12% | Charged on the seller's invoice, on the same base as IPRIMA |
| IPRIMA (first registration) | 20% | Five-seat car, SUV, or pickup. Higher for vans and buses |
| Shipping from the US | $1,050 to $3,900 | Roll-on/roll-off is cheapest, a shared container costs more |
| Customs broker | $500 to $1,000 | Files with SAT and secures the port release |
| Port handling | $150 to $400 | Plus storage if your paperwork stalls |
| First plates | About Q75 | Issued after the vehicle clears customs |

The taxable base is the original invoice from the foreign seller, not the shipped CIF value, as long as SAT can verify that invoice and you document the payment through the banking system. If it cannot, SAT falls back to its annual valuation table, which is the only place model year changes your bill.

A worked example makes it concrete. A used sedan invoiced at $15,000 owes $1,800 in IVA and $3,000 in IPRIMA, for a total tax of $4,800, or 32 percent. Add shipping and a broker and the car lands near $19,800, about Q151,000 before it is registered.

## Why electric and hybrid imports cost far less

Electric and hybrid vehicles pay IPRIMA at 5 percent instead of 20 percent, and Decreto 40-2022 adds further relief for clean-fuel imports. On the same $15,000 invoice, the first-registration tax drops from $3,000 to $750, cutting the total tax burden to roughly $2,550, or about 17 percent. If you are shopping for a car to bring down, a hybrid usually wins on the tax math alone.

There is also no model-year import ban. Guatemala will clear a 15-year-old car as readily as a new one, so age is a practical concern, not a legal wall.

## Documents you need to clear customs

- The original certificate of title. Never ship a copy.
- The seller's invoice, legally valid where it was issued.
- The bill of lading from the shipping line.
- Your passport and your NIT, the Guatemalan tax identification number.
- A power of attorney naming your customs broker (agente aduanal).

The original title matters more than anything else on that list. If a copy arrives instead, the car sits at the port accumulating storage of $15 to $50 per day while you courier the real document from abroad.

## Step by step: importing a car into Guatemala

1. **Compare the numbers first.** Price the same model locally before you commit. If the local price is close, skip the import.
2. **Buy the car and secure the original title and invoice.** Keep every receipt.
3. **Ship it** to Puerto Quetzal or Santo Tomás de Castilla.
4. **Hire a customs broker** who works the port you chose.
5. **The broker files with SAT** and pays the IVA and IPRIMA on your behalf.
6. **Inspect and release** the vehicle from customs.
7. **Plate and register it,** then budget for the annual circulation tax.

## Buying a car in Guatemala instead of importing

Used car prices in Guatemala run similar to or slightly higher than US prices, because import tax is baked into every vehicle and the supply is thinner. What you buy locally is convenience: no shipping, no port, no broker, and plates in the same week.

Two rules protect that purchase. First, file the traspaso, the ownership transfer, within 30 business days of the sale to avoid a fine. Second, verify the vehicle carries no outstanding lien through the Registro de la Propiedad before you pay. Popular and easy to service locally are the Toyota Hilux, Suzuki Swift, and Hyundai Tucson.

## What it costs to own and drive a car here

- **Annual circulation tax:** the ISCV and tarjeta de circulación are paid online through SAT's Agencia Virtual, in a window that runs from January 1 to July 31. Decreto 19-2024 trimmed the control vehicular charge for 2026.
- **Insurance:** collision and theft cover is optional but worth pricing on a newer import.
- **Fuel:** roughly Q33 to Q38 per gallon for regular.
- **Maintenance:** cobblestones and unpaved roads wear suspension and tires faster than flat highways.
- **Parking:** many colonial homes in the historic center have no off-street space, so a garage or secure lot is a real budget line and a real factor when you buy a house.

## Common mistakes expats make

- Shipping a photocopied title and paying weeks of port storage as a result.
- Assuming their residency exemption covers the car. It does not.
- Ignoring SAT's valuation table, then paying tax on a value higher than the price they negotiated.
- Forgetting that a colonial home may have no parking, then renting a space downtown for years.
- Buying locally and missing the 30-day traspaso deadline, which triggers a penalty.
- Choosing a car for the drive to Lake Atitlán without checking that local mechanics stock its parts.

Sort the paperwork before the car ships and the whole process is dull, which is exactly what you want from customs. Start with the budget, confirm the tax base, and use a broker who clears vehicles every week.
