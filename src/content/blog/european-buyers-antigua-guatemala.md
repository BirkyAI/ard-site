Antigua Guatemala has long been a magnet for North American buyers, but a growing number of European investors are discovering this colonial gem. British retirees, Spanish digital nomads, and German families are all finding their way to the foothills of Volcan de Agua.

If you are a European buyer considering property in Antigua Guatemala, this guide covers what you need to know -- from visa pathways and financing to the best neighborhoods for your lifestyle.

## Why European Buyers Are Choosing Antigua Guatemala

Several factors are driving European interest in Antigua's real estate market. The most compelling is value. A colonial home or modern apartment in Antigua costs a fraction of what you would pay for comparable property in London, Madrid, or Berlin. That price gap, combined with Guatemala's warm climate and rich culture, makes Antigua an attractive option for buyers seeking a second home, retirement property, or investment.

Currency exchange also works in favor of European buyers. The euro and British pound stretch further in Guatemala, where the quetzal remains stable against the US dollar. For buyers coming from countries with strong currencies, the purchasing power in Antigua's market is excellent.

## Visa Options for European Citizens

One of the biggest advantages for European buyers is Guatemala's straightforward visa system. EU and UK citizens can enter Guatemala visa-free and stay for up to 90 days. For longer stays, several options are available.

The temporary residency permit is popular among retirees and investors. You can qualify by showing sufficient monthly income (around $1,000 per month from a pension or investments) or by making a significant investment in Guatemalan real estate. The exact threshold varies, but buying property valued at $50,000 or more strengthens your application significantly.

For those looking to work remotely while living in Antigua, the digital nomad visa is a newer option. Guatemala introduced this visa to attract remote workers, and it is valid for up to two years. European freelancers and remote employees find this route particularly appealing because it requires no local employer sponsorship.

## Financing and Property Purchase for European Buyers

The process of buying property in Guatemala as a European citizen is straightforward. Foreigners have the same property rights as Guatemalan citizens, with one exception: you cannot own land within 15 kilometers of the border or coastline. Since Antigua is inland, this restriction does not apply.

For financing, most European buyers pay in cash or arrange financing through their home country bank. Guatemalan banks offer mortgages to foreign buyers, but the process takes longer and interest rates are higher than in Europe. A typical mortgage from a Guatemalan bank requires a 30 to 50 percent down payment and charges 8 to 12 percent interest.

The purchase process involves a notary (notario) who acts as both lawyer and closing agent. The notary conducts a title search, drafts the purchase agreement, and registers the property in your name. Expect closing costs of 3 to 5 percent of the purchase price, which covers the notary fee, property registration, and transfer tax.

## Best Neighborhoods for European Buyers

European buyers tend to gravitate toward different neighborhoods depending on their lifestyle. Here is a breakdown of popular areas.

**El Hato** is a mountain community just 15 minutes above Antigua. It attracts European buyers who want nature, space, and panoramic volcano views. The area has a growing community of expats from across Europe, and properties here range from modern eco-houses to rustic cabins. See our [El Hato rental properties](/properties/) for examples of available homes.

**Ciudad Vieja** offers a quieter, more traditional colonial experience. It is more affordable than central Antigua and attracts European buyers looking for a slower pace of life. Many Spanish and British retirees choose this area for its authentic feel and lower property prices.

**Jocotenango** is popular among families and younger European buyers. It has good schools, modern amenities, and a more suburban feel while still being walking distance from central Antigua. The mix of colonial homes and newer construction appeals to buyers who want modern conveniences with colonial charm.

**Central Antigua** (the historic core) attracts European buyers who want to be in the heart of the action. Properties here are more expensive and often require renovation, but the payoff is living steps from Antigua's main plaza, restaurants, and cultural events.

## What European Buyers Should Know About Daily Life

Living in Antigua Guatemala as a European expat is generally smooth, but there are a few things to know before you move.

**Healthcare.** Antigua has several private clinics with English-speaking doctors. Many European expats maintain health insurance in their home country and supplement it with a local Guatemalan plan. The private healthcare system is good and costs significantly less than in Europe for out-of-pocket visits.

**Language.** While you can get by with English in many settings, learning Spanish makes a huge difference. Antigua has dozens of excellent Spanish language schools, and many European expats take a one-month intensive course when they arrive.

**Banking.** Opening a Guatemalan bank account as a European citizen requires a passport, proof of income, and a reference letter from your home bank. Some banks require a residence visa, so check with individual banks before applying. HSBC and Banco Industrial are popular choices for expats.

**Internet and connectivity.** Antigua has reliable fiber internet in most areas. Speeds of 100 to 200 Mbps are standard, which is sufficient for remote work, video calls, and streaming. El Hato and other mountain communities have slower options, so check connectivity before committing to a property.
