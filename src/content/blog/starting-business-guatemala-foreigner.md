## Why Start a Business in Guatemala?

Guatemala offers several advantages for foreign entrepreneurs. The cost of labor and rent is significantly lower than in the United States, Canada, or Europe. The country uses the US dollar alongside the Quetzal for many commercial transactions, which simplifies accounting for American business owners. Guatemala's central location in the Americas makes it a natural hub for regional trade, and the country has a growing digital infrastructure in cities like Antigua and Guatemala City.

The expat community in Antigua is particularly welcoming to new business owners. There is a thriving network of entrepreneurs, co-working spaces, and local service providers who understand the needs of foreign investors. Many expats run successful businesses in tourism, real estate, hospitality, restaurants, digital marketing, and education.

## Visa Options for Business Owners

Before you start a business in Guatemala as a foreigner, you need to understand your visa options. You cannot work or operate a business on a tourist visa (90 days).

**Temporary Resident Visa (Residencia Temporal):** This is the most common visa for foreign business owners. You can apply after demonstrating a stable monthly income of at least $1,500 USD or by making a significant investment in the country (typically $50,000+ in real estate or business capital). The residency process takes 6-12 months and requires a local lawyer.

**Digital Nomad Visa (Visitante Temporal):** Guatemala introduced a digital nomad visa in 2024 allowing remote workers to stay for up to two years. While this visa is designed for remote employees, it can be a stepping stone while you establish your business.

**Permanent Residency (Residencia Permanente):** After two years of temporary residency, you can apply for permanent residency. This requires continued proof of income or business operations and a clean legal record.

**Investor Visa:** For those investing $50,000 USD or more in a Guatemalan business or real estate, the investor visa path is faster and more straightforward. You will need to demonstrate the investment and present a business plan.

## Business Entity Types in Guatemala

Choosing the right business structure is a critical step when starting a business in Guatemala as a foreigner. The most common options are:

**Sociedad Anónima (S.A.):** This is the most common corporate structure, similar to a US corporation or LLC. It requires at least two shareholders, a minimum capital of Q5,000 (about $650 USD), and must be registered with the Mercantile Registry. An S.A. provides limited liability protection and is the preferred structure for most foreign-owned businesses.

**Sociedad de Responsabilidad Limitada (S. de R.L.):** Similar to an LLC, this structure is suitable for smaller businesses with fewer than 20 partners. It offers limited liability but is less common for foreign investors because of restrictions on share transfers.

**Individual Enterprise (Empresa Individual):** A sole proprietorship option for small, low-risk businesses. The owner has unlimited personal liability, so this is generally not recommended for foreign entrepreneurs.

**Branch Office (Sucursal):** If you already have an established business abroad, you can register a Guatemalan branch. This is common for international companies expanding into Central America.

## Step-by-Step Registration Process

The process of starting a business in Guatemala as a foreigner involves several steps. With a good local lawyer, it typically takes 4-8 weeks.

**1. Hire a Guatemalan lawyer.** This is not optional. Foreigners must have legal representation for business registration. A good corporate lawyer will handle the entire process for a fee of $500-$1,500 USD depending on complexity.

**2. Reserve your company name.** Your lawyer will check the Mercantile Registry (Registro Mercantil) for name availability and reserve your chosen name. This costs approximately Q100 ($13 USD).

**3. Draft the incorporation deed.** Your lawyer prepares the public deed (escritura pública) specifying the company name, purpose, capital, share structure, and management. This is signed before a notary public.

**4. Register with the Mercantile Registry.** The deed is submitted to the Registro Mercantil for official registration. This step takes 2-4 weeks and costs around Q1,000 ($130 USD).

**5. Obtain a Tax ID (NIT).** Your company receives a Número de Identificación Tributaria from the SAT (Superintendencia de Administración Tributaria). This is needed for all tax filings, invoices, and bank accounts.

**6. Register with the Municipality.** Depending on your business location, you need a municipal business license (patente de comercio). In Antigua, this costs approximately Q500-Q2,000 ($65-$260 USD) depending on the business type.

**7. Open a corporate bank account.** You will need your NIT, company registration documents, and proof of address to open a business bank account. Banks in Guatemala include Banco Industrial, G&T Continental, and Banrural.

## Tax Obligations for Foreign-Owned Businesses

Guatemala's tax system is straightforward for businesses. The main taxes are:

**ISR (Income Tax):** 25% on net profits. Businesses can choose between the simplified regime (7% on gross revenue up to Q30,000/month) or the general regime (25% on net profit).

**IVA (VAT):** 12% on all goods and services. Your business charges IVA to customers and remits it to the SAT monthly. You can deduct IVA on business expenses.

**Annual Tax on Assets (ISO):** 1% of net assets, which is creditable against income tax.

**Reporting:** Monthly tax filings are required, submitted through the SAT's online portal. You will need a local accountant (contador) to handle filings. Expect to pay $100-$300 per month for accounting services.

## Hiring Employees in Guatemala

If your business grows and you need to hire, Guatemala's labor laws are worker-friendly. Key requirements include:

**Employment contracts:** Written contracts are mandatory for all employees. They specify salary, working hours, benefits, and termination terms.

**Minimum wage:** As of 2026, the minimum wage is approximately Q3,500 ($460 USD) per month for non-agricultural work. Most professional positions pay significantly more.

**Mandatory benefits:** Employees receive the 13th month bonus (aguinaldo) in December, the 14th month bonus (bono 14) in July, a yearly vacation bonus, and 30 days of paid vacation after one year of service.

**Severance:** After one year of employment, employees are entitled to severance pay of 30 days per year of service.

## Practical Tips for Foreign Entrepreneurs

**Learn the language.** While many professionals in Antigua speak English, government offices, banks, and the SAT operate in Spanish. Hire a bilingual lawyer and accountant, but invest in learning Spanish too.

**Work with trusted professionals.** Ask other expat business owners for referrals. A bad lawyer or accountant can cost you months of delays. The Antigua Real Estate Development team can connect you with trusted legal and accounting professionals.

**Understand the culture.** Guatemalan business culture is relationship-based. Meetings often start with small talk, and decisions take time. Patience and personal connections matter more than efficiency.

**Plan for cash flow.** Bank transfers and payments can take longer than in the US or Europe. Keep a cash buffer of 2-3 months of operating expenses.

**Consider your location.** Antigua is ideal for tourism, hospitality, real estate, and service businesses. Guatemala City is better for manufacturing, logistics, and wholesale distribution. El Hato and the surrounding villages offer lower rent and a quieter lifestyle.
