Yes, you can buy a property in Antigua Guatemala whose title is still in the name of someone who has died, but you are not buying a house in the ordinary sense. You are buying into a legal process. The safe route is to wait until the estate is settled and the property registered in the heirs' names. It comes up often here, because many Antigua houses for sale are family homes bought decades ago.

## Why do so many Antigua homes sit in a dead owner's name?

A house bought when land around Antigua was cheap stays in one family for two or three generations, the owner dies without a will, and Guatemalan law divides the property among forced heirs, usually the spouse and the children. Add heirs abroad, heirs who disagree, and a family decision to sort it out later. Later can run ten years.

## Can you legally buy a property if the owner has died?

Only the registered owner, or the heirs once registered, can transfer a property. While the file is open, no single person can sign a valid deed. A buyer has three routes.

| Route | What you are really buying | Who carries the risk | Honest verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Wait for the succession to finish, then buy from the heirs | A house registered in the heirs' names | Nobody unusual: an ordinary sale | The only route we recommend |
| A conditional promesa with a deposit | A promise to sell, conditional on the file finishing | You: no escrow, the deposit is the risk point | Workable with a deadline and money held by the notario |
| One heir's derechos y acciones | A share of an unfinished estate, not a house | You: paperwork, costs and any dispute | Speculative, not for a home to live in |

The first route needs patience and little else. The second is where foreign buyers lose deposits, because nobody wrote down what happens if the file takes two years. The third is a bet on the heirs and the courts.

## What does a succession involve in Guatemala?

An uncontested estate normally runs through a notario, not a court. The file moves step by step: the acta de requerimiento, notice to the Registro de Procesos Sucesorios, edictos calling the junta de herederos about fifteen days later, the junta itself, a fiscal appraisal by DICABI or by a valuador authorised by the Ministry of Finance, an opinion from the Procuraduría General de la Nación, and the auto declarativo de herederos naming the heirs and their shares. After the fiscal liquidation, testimonios are registered and notices go to DICABI and the municipal catastro. A contested estate, or one that cannot be handled notarially, goes to court instead. Where a testamento exists, the albacea named in the will leads the testamentaría.

## How long does a succession take in Antigua Guatemala?

A clean intestate file with cooperative heirs living locally commonly takes four to nine months from the acta de requerimiento to a testimonio registered in the heirs' names. A contested file, minor heirs, heirs abroad without a power of attorney, or a judicial process can run two years or more. Four things decide it: the edicto period and the PGN opinion, whether the appraisal is challenged, the registry backlog, and whether the heirs cooperate.

Ask the notario for a written estimate of the remaining steps: a seller who cannot show one usually has a file that has not really started. Our guide to [the closing process in Guatemala](/blog/guatemala-real-estate-closing-process/) shows what the final sale looks like once the title is clean.

## Who owns the house while the file is open?

The estate, in the heirs' hands, but nobody can sell it. One point catches buyers out constantly: under the comunidad regime the surviving spouse already owns half the marriage property by gananciales, so only the deceased's half passes through the succession. A widow or widower cannot sell the whole house alone. Minor heirs cannot sell their share without judicial authorisation, and the judge reviews the price being paid.

Adult heirs who live abroad can sign through a [power of attorney](/blog/power-of-attorney-guatemala-property/), and we cover boundary and plan problems in [property boundaries and land disputes](/blog/property-boundaries-land-disputes-guatemala/).

## What does the succession and the sale cost?

Start with the appraisal. A registered perito valuador charges roughly Q1,500 to Q5,000 for a standard Antigua home, and the fiscal appraisal for a succession is valid for five years, as in [how appraisals work in Guatemala](/blog/property-appraisal-valuation-guatemala/). Add the notary's fee, the registry entries, and the fiscal step the notario handles. Ask two questions in writing: the fee for the file, and the total cost of having the property registered in the heirs' names.

Then the purchase: transfer tax of about 3 percent of the registered value, notary fees of 1 to 2 percent, registry fees near 0.5 percent, legal review of $500 to $1,500, and a survey at Q3,000 to Q12,000 if the plan is old. Budget 5 to 7 percent of the price in closing costs, plus $250 to $400 for an appraisal if you finance.

## What should you verify before you sign anything?

- A title search at the Registro de la Propiedad, ideally back 15 years, checking for hipotecas and usufructo.
- A search of the Registro de Procesos Sucesorios for a registered testamento.
- The heirs and their exact shares on paper, including the spouse's half and any minor heirs.
- Whether IUSI and the municipal records are current, and who pays them.
- Who is living in the house: family, a tenant, or nobody.
- The plano against the walls, and whether a survey is needed.

## Red flags to walk away from

A seller who says the paperwork is almost done but cannot name the notario or show a file. A single heir offering to sign for a house with three heirs. A deposit requested against an unfinished succession, with no escrow, no deadline and no penalty clause. A venta de derechos presented as if it were the house. Pressure to skip the title search.

## What does the closing look like once the estate is settled?

All heirs sign before the notario, in person or through powers of attorney, and the escritura goes to the Registro de la Propiedad. Registration normally takes 15 to 30 business days, and the notario holds the funds until it confirms the transfer. Then you update IUSI and take over the maintenance. An inherited property in Antigua Guatemala can be an excellent purchase, often better than a renovated listing, because you buy from a family rather than a flipper.
