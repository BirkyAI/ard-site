Thinking of moving to Antigua Guatemala with your family? You are not alone. A growing number of expat families are choosing this colonial gem for its safety, warm community, and quality of life. But relocating with children brings questions that single travelers and couples rarely consider: Are there good schools? Is the city kid-friendly? Where should we live?

This guide covers everything you need to know about family life in Antigua Guatemala, from international schools to weekend activities and the best neighborhoods for raising children.

## International Schools in Antigua Guatemala

Education is usually the top priority for families considering a move abroad. Antigua Guatemala offers several quality options for international and bilingual education:

**Colegio Americano de Guatemala (CAG)** is the most well-known international school in the region. Located just outside Antigua in the neighboring town of Ciudad Vieja, it follows a US-based curriculum with instruction in English. CAG serves students from preschool through high school and is accredited by the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools. Many expat families choose CAG for its college preparatory program and extracurricular activities including sports, music, and arts.

**Colegio Capouilliez** has a campus in Antigua and offers a bilingual program (Spanish-English) from preschool through high school. Its curriculum blends the Guatemalan national program with an international focus, making it a popular choice for families who want their children to be fully bilingual.

**Liceo de Arte** focuses on a creative and holistic approach to education, with an emphasis on the arts alongside the standard academic curriculum. It offers preschool through middle school and is a good fit for families who value creativity and expression.

**Smaller bilingual schools** in Antigua and the surrounding villages (San Pedro Las Huertas, Jocotenango, and Santa Lucia Milpas Altas) offer more intimate learning environments with lower teacher-student ratios. These are often excellent options for younger children transitioning into a bilingual environment.

For families with toddlers and preschoolers, Antigua has a growing number of Montessori-inspired daycare centers and early education programs, many of which operate in English.

## Best Neighborhoods for Families

Where you live in Antigua makes a significant difference to your family's daily experience.

**Jocotenango**, just west of Antigua's historic center, is one of the most popular areas for expat families. It offers spacious homes with yards, quieter streets, and easy access to the central park. The neighborhood has its own market, pharmacy, and several family-friendly cafes. Many properties include private gardens where children can play safely.

**Ciudad Vieja** sits on Antigua's southern edge and is home to several international schools, making it a convenient choice for school drop-offs and pickups. It has a more suburban feel with larger lots and modern homes, and is particularly popular with families who plan to be in Antigua long term.

**San Pedro Las Huertas** and **Santa Lucia Milpas Altas** are villages just outside Antigua proper, offering a quieter, more rural lifestyle while still being a 10-15 minute drive from the city center. These areas tend to have larger properties with gardens and stunning volcano views. The trade-off is that you will need a car for most errands and school runs.

**El Hato** is an elevated mountain community a short drive from Antigua. The views are spectacular and properties often have generous outdoor space. Living here feels more connected to nature, with hiking trails and forested surroundings right outside your door. Families who value privacy and outdoor space often prefer El Hato over the more urban neighborhoods.

## Kid-Friendly Activities in Antigua

Antigua Guatemala is surprisingly rich in activities for children of all ages.

**Parque Central** is the heart of the city and a favorite spot for families. Children run around the fountain plaza, chase pigeons, and eat ice cream from street vendors. It is safe, central, and always lively on weekends. The nearby **Mercado de Artesanias** is fun for older children to explore.

**Cerro de la Cruz** offers a short uphill walk (or quick taxi ride) to a lookout point with panoramic views of the city framed by the volcanoes. Older children especially enjoy the adventure of getting there and the photo opportunities at the top.

**Chocolate and coffee tours** are surprisingly popular with kids. Several Antigua cafes offer child-friendly tours where children can see how chocolate is made from bean to bar. **Chocolate Museum** in the city center has interactive exhibits and tasting sessions that appeal to school-age children.

On weekends, **Finca El Tambor** in El Hato offers hiking trails, a small organic farm, and space for kids to run freely in nature. Many local families spend Sunday afternoons here exploring the trails and having picnics.

For rainy days (and Antigua has a distinct rainy season from May to October), many families enroll children in **art classes or music lessons** offered by local studios. The city has a thriving arts scene, and several expat-run programs offer English-language instruction.

## Healthcare and Safety for Families

Antigua Guatemala has several private clinics and hospitals that meet international standards. **Hospital de Antigua** and **Hospital Privado** both have pediatric services and emergency care. For more serious medical needs, Guatemala City's top hospitals are about an hour away by car.

Most expat families carry international health insurance, which is accepted at private facilities. Pharmacies are widely available, and many have English-speaking staff.

Safety is a common concern, and the reality is positive: Antigua Guatemala's historic center is one of the safest areas in Central America for families. The city has a strong police presence in tourist areas, and violent crime is rare. Most expat families feel comfortable walking their children to school, visiting the central park in the evening, and letting older children navigate the city independently.

That said, standard precautions apply: keep an eye on belongings in crowded markets, avoid poorly lit streets late at night, and lock doors and windows at home. Most expat homes have walled gardens and security gates, which provide an additional layer of security that families appreciate.

## Practical Considerations for Family Life

**Housing:** Most expat families rent a house or apartment rather than buying immediately. Rental properties with three or four bedrooms and a garden typically run from $1,000 to $2,500 per month, depending on location and size. Properties in El Hato and the villages tend to offer more space for the same budget.

**Getting around:** Many families own a car, and it is worth noting that Guatemalan traffic and driving styles take some getting used to. That said, within Antigua itself, most errands are walkable or a short tuk-tuk ride away.

**Grocery shopping:** Antigua has several supermarkets (La Torre, Super Del, Despensa Familiar) that stock most international brands. Specialty stores carry imported goods from the US and Europe. The local markets offer fresh produce at excellent prices, and many families buy their fruits and vegetables there weekly.

**Community:** The expat family community in Antigua is welcoming and active. Facebook groups, WhatsApp chats, and regular meetups make it easy to connect with other parents. Many families organize playdates, birthday parties, and holiday celebrations together.
