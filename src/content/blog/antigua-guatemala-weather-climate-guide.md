If you're thinking about buying property or renting in Antigua Guatemala, the weather is one of the first things you'll want to understand. And the good news? It's one of the best reasons to make the move.

Antigua Guatemala sits at 1,533 meters (5,030 feet) above sea level in a valley surrounded by three volcanoes: Agua, Fuego, and Acatenango. This elevation gives the city a temperate climate that locals call "eternal spring." Temperatures rarely dip below 12°C (54°F) or climb above 28°C (82°F). But the weather does have a rhythm, and understanding it helps you make smarter decisions about when to visit, when to buy, and what kind of property suits your lifestyle.

## Year-Round Temperature Overview

Unlike most of North America and Europe, Antigua doesn't have four distinct seasons. Instead, the year splits into two halves: dry season (November through April) and rainy season (May through October).

**Average temperatures by season:**

| Month Range | Avg High | Avg Low | Season |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Nov–Apr | 24–28°C (75–82°F) | 12–15°C (54–59°F) | Dry season |
| May–Oct | 22–26°C (72–79°F) | 14–17°C (57–63°F) | Rainy season |

The temperature difference between "winter" and "summer" is negligible. What changes is the rain, the humidity, and the amount of sun you'll see each day.

## Dry Season: November to April

This is Antigua's high season for tourism and real estate showings, and for good reason.

**What to expect:** Blue skies, bright sun, and cool mornings that warm up by mid-morning. The volcanoes are visible almost every day. Rain is rare, you might go weeks without a single drop. Evenings get chilly, especially from December through February, when temperatures can drop to 10–12°C (50–54°F).

**Best for property viewings:** The dry season is the ideal time to visit properties. Roads are easy to navigate, gardens are visible without overgrowth, and you can see the full extent of outdoor spaces. Roofs and drainage systems are easier to inspect when they're not being tested by rain.

**Peak months:** December, January, and February draw the most visitors. Properties in the best neighborhoods, like **El Hato** or **Santa Ana**, often get booked solid for short-term rentals during this period. If you're looking at rental income potential, these months deliver the highest nightly rates.

## Rainy Season: May to October

Don't let the name scare you. The rainy season in Antigua isn't constant downpour. It follows a predictable pattern that many long-term residents actually prefer.

**What to expect:** Mornings are usually sunny and clear. Rain arrives in the afternoon, typically between 2 PM and 6 PM, in short, heavy bursts. The skies clear again by evening. May and June have the heaviest rainfall. July and August see a slight break (a short dry spell locals call the *canícula*), then September and October bring the heaviest rains of the year.

**Advantages of the rainy season:**
- Greener landscapes, flowering gardens, and fuller trees
- Fewer tourists, more availability and lower prices on short-term rentals
- Cooler temperatures during the hottest part of the day
- Properties with good drainage and sound construction stand out immediately

**What to check during rainy season viewings:** If you're looking at properties in September or October, you get a built-in stress test. Look for signs of moisture in the walls, check that windows seal properly, and ask about drainage in the patio or garden. Colonial homes with old tile roofs may need maintenance, the rainy season will reveal any issues.

## How Weather Affects Property Decisions

Your choice of property should account for Antigua's microclimates. The city is compact, but different areas feel different depending on the season.

**Ground-floor vs. upper-floor.** In colonial homes with thick walls, ground-floor rooms stay naturally cool year-round. Upper floors get more direct sunlight and can be warmer in the dry season. If you're sensitive to cool mornings, look for a property with a sunny interior courtyard or a south-facing terrace.

**Patios and gardens.** Outdoor space is a major selling point in Antigua, but it behaves differently by season. A shaded patio is usable year-round. An open, sun-exposed garden is glorious in the dry season but may be too hot by mid-afternoon. Covered terraces and pergolas extend your outdoor living space well into the rainy season.

**Heating and cooling.** Most Antigua homes don't have central heating or air conditioning. You won't need AC, the natural temperature is comfortable. But you may want a fireplace or space heater for December and January mornings. Properties built in the last 10 years often include passive solar design elements that keep indoor temperatures stable.

## Best Time to Visit for Property Buyers

If you're flying in from abroad to look at properties, aim for **January through March** or **November**. These months give you the best weather for the widest property search. You'll be able to see the city at its most beautiful, inspect properties without weather complications, and still find availability at hotels and short-term rentals.

But if you're serious about understanding what you're buying, **come back in September**, or at least ask your real estate agent for photos and video taken during the rainy season. A property that looks great in February may reveal drainage or moisture issues in September. A good agent will be honest about both.

## What to Pack for Your Property Search

- **Dry season (Nov–Apr):** Light layers. A t-shirt or blouse for midday, a sweater or light jacket for mornings and evenings. Closed-toe shoes for walking on cobblestone streets.
- **Rainy season (May–Oct):** Same light layers, plus a compact umbrella or light rain jacket. Waterproof footwear is a smart choice, cobblestones get slippery when wet.
- **Year-round:** Sunscreen and sunglasses. The UV index is high at 1,533 meters elevation regardless of cloud cover.

## The Bottom Line

Antigua Guatemala's climate is one of its strongest selling points. You can enjoy outdoor cafes, rooftop terraces, and garden patios almost every day of the year. The rainy season brings afternoon showers that keep the valley green and the air clean, and the dry season delivers the kind of sunshine that makes you forget winter exists.

Whether you're buying a colonial home in **San Pedro** or building new construction in **Jocotenango**, the weather is a feature, not a concern. And if you time your visit right, you'll experience the best of both seasons.
