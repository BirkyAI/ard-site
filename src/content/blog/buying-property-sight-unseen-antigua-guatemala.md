# Buying Property in Antigua Guatemala Sight-Unseen

Buyers close on Antigua Guatemala houses every month without standing in the living room first. Nothing in Guatemalan law requires you to appear in person, and the Registro de la Propiedad only cares that the deed is drafted, stamped and submitted correctly. What remote buying demands is that you replace presence with process: local eyes on the ground, an independent inspection, a registry search, and a closing sequence that keeps your deposit protected until title is confirmed. Skip those, and distance stops being a convenience and becomes the risk.

## Can you really buy a property in Antigua Guatemala without visiting it first?

Yes. The escritura can be signed by a lawyer holding your power of attorney, which you grant at home and have apostilled. The notary, not you, walks the paperwork through the Registro, and registration takes roughly 15 to 30 business days. Plenty of foreign buyers in Antigua live in the United States, Canada or Europe and complete the purchase remotely.

What you cannot delegate is judgement. A person standing in a corridor at four in the afternoon learns things a camera never shows, so the whole discipline of sight-unseen buying is knowing what can be verified at a distance, what cannot, and how to price that gap. If you would rather look before you leap, the [properties](/properties/) we list are all viewable in person, and a single trip answers more questions than a year of WhatsApp calls.

## What a remote buyer can genuinely verify from abroad

The list is longer than most buyers expect, as long as the checks are done by someone who is not being paid by the seller.

- **Live video, not an edited reel.** Ask for a scheduled WhatsApp or Zoom call in which you direct the camera. Walk it to the corners, open the taps, look up at the ceiling.
- **The legal identity of the property.** The folio real at the Registro de la Propiedad shows the registered owner, the property's measurements and any lien, mortgage or usufruct attached to it.
- **Tax standing.** The municipal office can confirm whether IUSI property tax and any local charges are current. Unpaid tax follows the property, not the seller.
- **Water.** Ask whether the house is on municipal supply, has a well, a tinaco or a cistern, and how many hours a day water actually arrives.
- **Condition, by a paid professional.** A 2026 independent inspection runs $150 to $450 depending on size and covers structure, roof, plumbing, electrical, moisture and finishes.
- **Measurements against the plano.** The deed says "más o menos" more often than anyone likes. Have the figures on the plano checked against what is fenced.
- **The street, at the wrong hours.** Ask for photos and a short video at 7am and at 10pm on a weekday, not at golden hour on a Sunday.
- **Adjoining plots.** Satellite imagery plus a registry check on the neighbours tells you whether the empty lot next door is zoned for a four-storey building.

## What no video can tell you

Be honest about the blanks. A camera does not carry smell, so damp in a north-facing corridor, a weak drain or a sewer line that gurgles will not reach you. Microphones do not carry acoustics: how loud the street gets when a bus passes, whether the church bells are charming or relentless at six in the morning. Water pressure looks fine on video and collapses at peak hours. The most expensive hidden item is almost always the roof, which behaves differently in July than in January.

If any of those matter to you, and they usually do, budget an inspection pass after the rainy season rather than before it. Every remote purchase has at least one blind spot, so aim to make it cheap rather than pretend it does not exist.

## What remote due diligence costs in 2026

| Item | 2026 cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent inspection report | $150 to $450 | Two to four hours on site, paid by you, owed to you |
| Registry title search (folio real) | Q800 to Q2,500 | Ordered through a notary or attorney |
| Survey and deslinde, if boundaries are unclear | Q3,000 to Q12,000 | Licensed surveyor, weeks not days |
| Power of attorney, granted abroad and registered | Roughly $300 to $700 | Two to four weeks door to door |
| Transfer tax (impuesto de alcaldía) | 3% of registered value | Split buyer and seller by agreement |
| Property registry fee | About 0.5% of registered value | Paid on registration |

Total it before you fall in love with a listing. Roughly two to four percent of the price, on top of the price itself, is normal for a remote closing.

## The safest sequence when you cannot travel

1. **Agree price and terms in writing** before money moves, even a WhatsApp summary you both confirm.
2. **Order the inspection and the registry search first.** These are the two reports that end bad deals early and cheaply.
3. **Sign a promesa de venta with the conditions spelled out.** State what happens if the title is not clean, and set a date.
4. **Keep the deposit small and conditional.** Five to ten percent is normal. Make the contract say the deposit returns if title fails.
5. **Grant the power of attorney.** Sign before a notary at home, apostille it, then have it protocolised and registered in Guatemala.
6. **Close, register, and read the testimonio.** Confirm registration is in your name before releasing final funds.

## Why the deposit is the step that matters most

Guatemala has no escrow company standing between buyer and seller the way the United States does. Deposits frequently go straight into a seller's or an agent's personal account, which is exactly where remote buyers get hurt. Three habits prevent almost all of it. Keep the deposit modest. Write the refund condition into the promesa. And when the amount is large, ask for the funds to sit with the notary rather than the seller.

## When it is worth flying in anyway

Come down if the price is above roughly $300,000, if the house is colonial or inside the heritage zone and you have structural questions, if you plan a major renovation, if the seller refuses an independent inspection, or if you intend to rent it out, because rental value is set by the street, not by the house.
