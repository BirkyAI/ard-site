Smart home technology in Antigua Guatemala works, but only if you plan it around two local realities: a power grid that cuts out, and a colonial shell with no cavity in the wall to hide a cable. The upgrades that earn their keep here are the unglamorous ones: mesh Wi-Fi, camera coverage, a small battery backup and a water alarm. Most imported gadgetry is a novelty you stop using after a month.

## Which smart home upgrades are actually worth it in Antigua Guatemala?

Four, in order. A mesh Wi-Fi network that reaches the back patio, because one router behind a 60 cm wall covers one room. A camera system that records to a local drive, so it keeps working when the internet drops. A small uninterruptible power supply for the router, the modem and two lights, so the house stays connected and lit through a cut. And a water alarm under the tinaco or the kitchen sink, because a hidden leak inside a thick wall is the most expensive repair a colonial house can hand you. Everything else, voice assistants, smart bulbs in every socket, an app for the coffee machine, does not solve an Antigua problem.

## Why does the local power and internet shape every device choice?

Guatemala's grid runs at 120 volts and 60 hertz, and most Antigua homes are wired 120/240 on two legs, so US devices plug in while European 230 volt appliances with motors or clocks run wrong, the point we cover in [our guide to electrical system upgrades](/blog/electrical-system-upgrades-antigua-guatemala-homes/). Outages are frequent enough to plan around, as we explain in [power outages and backup solutions](/blog/guatemala-power-outages-backup-solutions/), and a device that needs the cloud becomes a paperweight the moment the router loses power. Choose devices that keep working locally, with a physical button and a hub inside the house, and treat app control as a bonus.

## How do you keep smart devices alive through a power cut?

Put the router, the modem and the camera recorder on a UPS, and add one lamp on the same circuit so you are not searching for a torch. Choose switches that fall back to a physical press, not ones that only respond through an app, and prefer a hub in the house over devices that talk straight to a foreign server. If you already run an inverter or a battery system, move the network gear onto it and leave the big loads off, as in the [power backup guide](/blog/guatemala-power-outages-backup-solutions/).

## Is a smart security setup worth it in Antigua Guatemala?

Yes, and it is the upgrade buyers ask about most. A four-camera system covering the porton, the street facade and the courtyard, recording to a local drive, covers the realistic cases, and smart locks suit rentals where guest codes beat a key handover. What matters is placement and the permit line: a camera or video intercom on the street facade is a visible change to the front of the house and falls under the municipal licence and CNPAG opinion we set out in [building permits](/blog/building-permits-antigua-guatemala/), while anything on the courtyard or rear wall is out of scope. Our [home security guide](/blog/home-security-antigua-guatemala/) covers the rest, and [walls, gates and fences](/blog/walls-gates-fences-antigua-guatemala/) covers the gate motors and intercoms these systems connect to.

## Can smart devices help with water?

They help more than people expect, because water, not crime, is the quiet risk in an old Antigua house. A leak sensor under the tinaco, the pump and the sink alarms before a drip becomes a wall. A tank level sensor tells you the tinaco is low before the shower does, and a pump controller protects the pump from running dry. Pair these with the setup in [water storage and rainwater](/blog/water-storage-rainwater-antigua-guatemala/) and the system becomes visible from your phone, what an owner abroad needs.

## Does a colonial home make smart wiring harder?

It changes the method, not the outcome. The walls are 40 to 80 cm of adobe, brick or block with no cavity to chase, so new cable runs sit in surface conduit matched to the wall colour, the same constraint the [lighting guide](/blog/lighting-colonial-homes-antigua-guatemala/) describes. Wooden windows and thick walls also eat Wi-Fi, which is why a mesh network beats a single powerful router in almost every colonial house. Budget the conduit and routing as part of the job.

## Which devices are a waste of money here?

Anything that stops working without the internet: a cloud-only doorbell, a smart plug with no manual switch, an appliance whose only control is an app. Motorised blinds and smart thermostats rarely justify their cost in a climate where most homes use a fan, not air conditioning, as we explain in [cooling homes in Antigua](/blog/cooling-homes-antigua-guatemala/). And a battery doorbell camera is no substitute for the physical layers of a [secure house](/blog/home-security-antigua-guatemala/): a solid door, good locks and a wall.

## What should you check before buying a "smart" home?

Ask whether the devices need the cloud or run locally, and where the hub lives, and whether it survives a power cut. Check the Wi-Fi reaches the far bedroom and the patio, not just the living room. Confirm the cameras and the intercom are permitted where they sit. And if the seller keeps the account, make sure the devices transfer to you, the same handover question as for every built-in, in [what's included in a property sale](/blog/whats-included-property-sale-antigua-guatemala/).
