Antigua Guatemala is an easy place to work from home. Fiber internet reaches the centre and the ring villages, the climate means you rarely need heating or air conditioning to sit at a desk, and a colonial house almost always has one room to spare. What decides whether it works is the room you choose and two small upgrades: a connection that survives thick walls, and backup for the days the grid drops.

## Can you work from home in Antigua Guatemala?

Yes, comfortably. Most homes in and around the centre have fiber from Claro, Tigo or a local provider, delivering roughly 80 to 150 Mbps down, more than enough for video calls. The climate does the rest: days sit between 22 and 26 C all year, so a well-placed room stays pleasant with a fan and open shutters rather than air conditioning.

Antigua is also small enough to walk, so you can leave the desk and be at a café in five minutes. If you would rather not work at home at all, our guide to [remote work in Antigua](/blog/remote-work-antigua-guatemala/) covers the coworking spaces and cafés.

## Which room in a colonial house makes the best home office?

The best office opens onto the courtyard, not onto the street. Colonial houses here are a ring of rooms around a patio, and that layout decides everything. A patio-facing room gets daylight and quiet. A street-facing room gets cobblestone traffic, tuk-tuks, gas and water trucks, church bells and, in season, processions and cohetes you cannot block out.

Colonial fabric works in your favour too: walls of 40 to 80 cm hold a steady temperature and blunt outside noise, ceilings of 3 to 4 m let warm air rise, and small high windows give soft light without screen glare.

Two rooms to avoid: a windowless interior room, and a front room on a busy street. A corredor, the covered walkway around the patio, makes a fine fair-weather office, and a zaguán can take a small desk without costing you a bedroom.

## What does a home office setup cost in Antigua Guatemala?

You do not need to remodel a house. The list below is what a remote worker usually adds to an existing colonial room. Prices are installed, supply and labour, checked with local trades in November 2026 at about Q7.7 to the dollar.

## What should you check about internet before you buy?

Fiber availability is address-specific, even inside the historic centre, so confirm it for the exact street rather than for "Antigua"; ask the seller for their internet bill, or check the provider's coverage by street. In a colonial house the router is rarely the problem; the walls are. A single router in the zaguán will not reach a room across the patio, so plan a mesh system with a node in the office and keep a prepaid hotspot as a second connection, about Q100 to Q150 a month for 15 to 20 GB.

## How do you keep working when the power goes out?

Power cuts happen often enough here to plan for them, and a home office is the one place where a cut costs money. The cheapest fix is a UPS holding the router, modem and laptop through a short cut of one to two hours. Longer outages need an inverter or a generator; our guide to [power outages and backup solutions](/blog/guatemala-power-outages-backup-solutions/) explains the sizes. Do not leave the office on the same circuit as the water pump, or a busy morning will trip the breaker.

## How do you keep a home office quiet and comfortable?

Quiet is mostly about the room, not the fittings. Start with a patio-facing space. If the room sits next to the street, a solid interior wooden door (Q1,200 to Q3,000 installed) and a thermal-lined curtain (Q400 to Q1,500) cut most of the traffic noise; our [guide to noise in Antigua](/blog/noise-quiet-home-antigua-guatemala/) explains what can and cannot be blocked.

Then layer the light and the air. A single ceiling point in a 3.5 m room leaves you working in your own shadow, so add a desk lamp and a floor lamp on bulbs around 2,700 K; our post on [lighting a colonial home](/blog/lighting-colonial-homes-antigua-guatemala/) explains why the wiring costs more than the fittings. For temperature, a ceiling fan on an existing point (Q450 to Q1,500) is enough in almost every room; only a west-facing upper-floor room under teja justifies a 12,000 BTU minisplit (Q4,500 to Q9,000), as our [cooling guide](/blog/cooling-homes-antigua-guatemala/) explains.

## Do you need a dedicated room, or can you use the dining table?

You need a room with a door. The dining table works for a week and stops working once you take calls every day. The best setup is a spare bedroom or a study off the courtyard, but a corredor, a zaguán nook or a converted cuarto de servicio all work if they are yours and not the household's. Before you buy, ask how many rooms can stay in use at once.

## What should a remote-work buyer check before buying?

- A room that opens onto the courtyard, with a door you can close
- Fiber confirmed at the exact address, not just the neighbourhood
- Outlet count in the room, and a spare way in the panel
- A second bathroom, so a morning call does not depend on the household
- Mobile signal inside the room, since thick walls weaken it
- Street noise morning and evening, checked in person
- A courtyard or corredor for breaks

## Is Antigua a good long-term base for remote work?

Yes. It is mild, walkable, close to Guatemala City and the airport, and it has an established remote-work community. The cost of living is well below a city in North America or Europe, and a home with a real office costs a fraction of what the same setup costs there. Buy for the room rather than the view: a quiet patio-facing study is the feature you will use every day.
