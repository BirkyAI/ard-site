A typical home in Antigua Guatemala was asking between $196,000 and $653,000 in September 2026, with a median near $420,000 and a median asking price of about $2,549 per square metre of construction in the town proper. Inside that band, the number you pay is set less by the neighborhood name than by three things: how far you are from the Parque Central, how much of the house has already been restored, and what the road and the water do in the rainy season. This guide puts the real price bands side by side, area by area, so you can see where your budget actually lands.

## What does each area of Antigua Guatemala cost in 2026?

These are asking prices, not closings, because Guatemala keeps no public record of what properties actually sell for. Every figure below comes from the area guides we publish on this site, and we update them as the market moves.

| Area | Typical home price | What that buys |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Antigua historic centre (the casco) | $250,000 to $800,000 | A restored colonial around a courtyard, original detail, and the shortest walk to the Parque Central |
| Ciudad Vieja | $120,000 to $180,000 | A renovated three-bedroom colonial with a garden, roughly 20 to 40 percent below the centre |
| Jocotenango | $130,000 to $220,000 | A renovated colonial, or $180,000 to $280,000 for a modern four-bedroom, with small apartments from $50,000 |
| San Miguel Dueñas | $120,000 to $250,000 | A three-bedroom colonial, or $150,000 to $300,000 for new construction with volcano views |
| San Cristóbal El Alto and the hillside | $40,000 to $250,000 | View lots and hillside homes, the widest spread on this list |
| The ring villages | $80,000 to $200,000 | A village house with usable land, or an outlying village home or lot from $30,000 |
| El Hato and the coffee hills | $30,000 to $200,000 | Rural land from $15 to $50 per square metre, or a country house with a garden |

Two things stand out. The first is the size of the gap between the casco and everything around it. The centre carries roughly a 1.2 times premium over Ciudad Vieja measured per square metre of construction, and the further you move into the ring villages, the further that number falls. Measured house against house the gap is wider still, because the casco holds the trophy end of the market as well as the mid-range: the town-wide median sits near $420,000 and the top of the casco band runs well above it. The second is the spread inside a single area: the same street can hold a $250,000 shell and a $650,000 finished house, and almost all of the difference is restoration, not postcode.

## Why do two houses ten minutes apart differ by $400,000?

Three reasons, and none of them is the view.

**Restoration state.** Restoration runs $150 to $400 per square metre depending on how much is structural, and furnishing adds $15,000 to $50,000 on top. A raw shell and a finished house four doors apart are the same building on paper and a different product in practice.

**Heritage supply.** The historic core is a UNESCO World Heritage site. That status protects the architecture, and it also caps supply: you cannot tear down a colonial facade and build something taller or denser. Inside the grid, prices are set by how rarely anything comes up for sale. Outside it, they are set by land.

**Access and services.** A village ten minutes out that has mains water, a paved road and fibre internet prices very differently from a finca on a dirt track that needs its own well and a 4x4 in October. That infrastructure gap is the entire reason the [villages around Antigua](/blog/villages-around-antigua-guatemala/) exist as a cheaper market at all.

## Which area suits which kind of buyer?

- **Short walk, heritage, highest liquidity:** the casco. Expect $250,000 as the floor for a free-standing colonial, and $350,000 to $450,000 for a fully restored three-bedroom.
- **Best value on a restored courtyard house:** the ring villages and Ciudad Vieja, where the same square metres cost 20 to 40 percent less and you trade a walk for a short drive.
- **Rental income:** the sub-$200,000 condominium and small-apartment band still produces the strongest cash flow per dollar, especially on mid-term and winter rentals.
- **A modern home rather than a restoration project:** new construction outside the centre runs $80,000 to $300,000, and it is still the cheaper route to a modern house.
- **Land and space:** the coffee hills, where a buildable plot starts in the low tens of dollars per square metre and you provide your own water.

## What does land cost around Antigua Guatemala?

Land is priced more simply than houses, and location inside the valley drives almost all of it. A 500 square metre plot within walking distance of the centre typically lists between $80,000 and $150,000. The same 500 square metres in Jocotenango lists between $40,000 and $80,000. Outside the ring villages, on the road up towards El Hato and Pastores, rural land runs from $15 to $50 per square metre, and the deciding factor is how much it costs to bring water and power to the boundary.

## What do you pay on top of the purchase price?

Budget roughly 5 to 7 percent of the purchase price for closing costs, made up of notary fees, the municipal transfer tax and the property registry fee. The deposit is normally 10 percent, held in the notary's trust account, and banks will typically finance 60 to 70 percent of the appraised value for a foreign buyer. The full sequence is set out step by step in [our guide to the closing process](/blog/guatemala-real-estate-closing-process/).

After the purchase, plan for ownership costs: property management if you are not on the ground, insurance, IUSI, and a maintenance reserve. [What it costs to own a home here](/blog/cost-of-owning-home-antigua-guatemala/) sets out the annual figures, and they are the reason a cheaper village house is not always the cheaper house.

## How do you compare two listings fairly?

Before you compare a price, normalise it.

1. **Price per square metre of construction, not per property.** A $300,000 house that is 170 square metres is a different proposition from a $280,000 house that is 220 square metres.
2. **Check what is included.** Fixtures, appliances, water rights and furniture move the real price by tens of thousands, as we explain in [what is included in a property sale here](/blog/whats-included-property-sale-antigua-guatemala/).
3. **Read the restoration paperwork, not the photographs.** Ask for the date of the electrical and plumbing work. In this market, that is the difference between the two houses on the same street.
4. **Walk the specific property, not the neighborhood.** Orientation, noise, drainage and the noon light vary block by block, which is what our [property viewing checklist](/blog/property-viewing-checklist-antigua-guatemala/) is built around.
