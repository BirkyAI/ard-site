Antigua Guatemala is a good place to rent out a room in your own home. The colonial plan hands you the pieces: rooms around a patio, often a second bathroom you can give to a tenant, and usually a spare room with its own door to the courtyard. Between the language schools, the volunteers and interns, the medical and university students, and the long-stay visitors, demand for a furnished room is real. A single room is also far cheaper than an apartment, so you reach a wider pool of people than a whole-house rental would.

The honest version: renting a room is the smallest and most flexible rental play in the market, but it is not passive. You share a wall with your income, and the rules change depending on whether you rent by the month or by the night. Here is how it works in 2026.

## Can you rent out a room in your Antigua Guatemala home?

Yes. Nothing stops an owner from renting a room inside their own house, whether you live there or not, and it is common in the centre and the ring villages. You do not need residency to own the property or collect rent, and a foreign owner signs a lease the same way a Guatemalan does. What changes is which rulebook applies. Rent by the month to someone who shares the house and you are signing an ordinary lease under the Civil Code (Decreto Ley 106). Rent by the night and you are running a short-term rental, with its own municipal registration and reporting.

## What does a room earn in Antigua Guatemala in 2026?

A room is priced well below a whole apartment, which is why it fills quickly. A one-bedroom apartment in the centre rents for about $400 to $700 a month, and a furnished long-term rental averages near $877. A single room sits at a fraction of that, and a furnished room with its own bathroom earns the most.

## Which room works best to rent out?

The best room is one off the patio with its own door to the courtyard, so your tenant comes and goes without crossing your living space. The second-best feature is a private bathroom: without one, the room is harder to fill and harder to live with. Buy within walking distance of the language schools and the centre, where demand is. A ground-floor room is easier to rent and to adapt, the same logic that makes single-storey colonial homes good for [accessible living](/blog/accessible-living-antigua-guatemala/) and [multigenerational households](/blog/multigenerational-homes-antigua-guatemala/).

## How do you set up a room to rent?

- **Furnish it.** A furnished room rents for far more than an empty one, and the setup need not be expensive. See [what it costs to furnish a home](/blog/cost-to-furnish-home-antigua-guatemala/).
- **Add a bathroom if you can.** If the house has only one, a second small bathroom is the upgrade that unlocks the higher rent. Expect Q19,000 to Q58,000 for a full bathroom, or Q35,000 to Q90,000 to add one from scratch, per our [kitchen and bathroom renovation guide](/blog/kitchen-bathroom-renovation-antigua-guatemala/).
- **Give the room its own entrance and a lock.** A door to the patio plus a keyed lock is what makes sharing work.
- **Fix the internet and the utilities.** A monthly tenant needs reliable WiFi that reaches the room, so mesh nodes are usually the answer, as we set out in the [home office guide](/blog/home-office-antigua-guatemala/). Either include utilities and price the rent up, or install a submeter and bill separately.

## What rules apply when you rent a room?

A **monthly room rental** is an ordinary lease. Put it in writing, take a deposit of roughly one month, and use the 30-day notice that is the common standard. Registering the lease at the Registro de la Propiedad is what makes it enforceable against third parties. Vet the tenant properly: there is no credit score to pull, so verification is manual and in writing, as we explain in [how to screen a tenant](/blog/screening-tenants-antigua-guatemala/). If things go wrong, a desalojo runs only through a judge. The full set of owner rules is in [landlord and tenant rights in Guatemala](/blog/landlord-tenant-rights-guatemala/).

A **nightly room rental** is a short-term rental under a different rule. Register with the municipality and follow the short-term rental rules on occupancy caps and quiet hours before you list a single night. See [short-term rental regulations in Antigua Guatemala](/blog/short-term-rental-regulations-antigua-guatemala-2026/).

## Do you pay tax on room rental income?

Yes. Rental income is taxable, and a furnished short-term room can also attract IVA on top. Keep receipts, declare the income, and ask an accountant how to file it.

## Does renting a room affect your insurance or mortgage?

It can. Tell your insurer the home is used for rental, because a standard homeowner policy may not cover commercial or short-term rental use, and short-term use must be declared, as we note in the [property insurance guide](/blog/guatemala-property-insurance/). If you financed the purchase, read the loan terms before you let a room, and check whether the change of use affects your IUSI filing.

## What are the risks of renting a room?

The risks are real. You are sharing your home, so privacy and noise are part of the deal, and nightly guests mean turnover, cleaning and constant messages. A monthly tenant who stops paying is slow to remove, because desalojo is a court process. Your deposit is your main security, so hold it properly and photograph an inventory.

## What should you check before buying a home to rent a room?

- A second bathroom, or the space and plumbing to add one.
- A room with its own door to the patio.
- Whether the property sits in the short-term-rental-regulated zone.
- Internet that reaches the room through the walls.
