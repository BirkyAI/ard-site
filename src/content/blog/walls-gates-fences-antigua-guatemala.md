Almost every home in Antigua Guatemala sits behind a perimeter wall and a gate, and that wall is one of the first things a buyer needs to understand. A new block wall two metres high costs Q450 to Q900 per linear metre ($58 to $117), and an automatic sliding gate with its motor runs Q9,000 to Q22,000 ($1,170 to $2,850) installed. Inside the protected historic centre, anything built or changed facing the street needs approval before the first block is laid.

## How much does a perimeter wall cost in Antigua Guatemala?

These are 2026 figures from masons and metal shops in Antigua, Jocotenango and Ciudad Vieja, including labour and standard materials, on level ground with the line already defined.

| Work | Typical cost |
|---|---|

A three-metre stretch of wall, one gate and a motor is a Q15,000 to Q35,000 project, which is why most buyers inherit the wall rather than build it.

## What are perimeter walls actually made of here?

- **Block and cement render.** The default across the country. Cheap, quick, and it takes paint or lime wash without trouble.
- **Adobe and bahareque.** Common in older properties and in the villages. Breathable and beautiful, but a wall built this way needs a footing that keeps water out of the base, or the bottom of the wall softens through the wet season.
- **Volcanic stone or exposed block.** Used on the wall that faces the street, at roughly 40 to 60 percent above plain block.
- **Iron railing and reja.** Set on a low wall or in place of one. Open, cheaper than masonry above about two metres, and it rusts, so it needs paint every three to four years.

## What does a gate cost, and which type should you choose?

A sliding gate is the default for a driveway. It needs a clear run of its own width alongside the opening, so it suits a wide frontage on flat ground. A swing gate suits a narrow frontage, but both leaves have to clear the street as they open, and that is the arrangement that ends up in an argument with a parked car. A manual gate on good rollers is the cheapest option and lasts decades if the track is set level. The motor is a separate line item, and it needs a battery, a control board and roughly one service a year.

Two practical points. Do not motorise a gate that already hangs badly: fix the track and the rollers first, because a motor pushing a dragging gate burns itself out. And keep a manual override with a key, because power cuts are routine and a gate you cannot open from the inside is a trap.

## Can you build or change a wall in the historic centre?

Inside the protected area the street facade is treated as part of the city, not as private decoration. The [heritage rules that apply to Antigua properties](/blog/antigua-guatemala-unesco-heritage-property/) put height, materials, colour and the shape of the opening under review, and permission comes from the municipality with the opinion of the heritage authority, CNPAG. Reproducing what is already there, same height, same material, same colour, is the least regulated option and normally the fastest to approve. A taller wall, a change of material or a modern gate design on a street facade is the change most likely to be refused.

Outside the protected area, in Jocotenango, San Pedro Las Huertas, El Hato, Ciudad Vieja or a gated compound, a new wall normally needs a municipal construction licence. In [gated communities](/blog/gated-communities-antigua-guatemala/) the perimeter wall, the gate and the guard post are usually already built and shared, so read the rules on what you may attach to the outer wall before you plan anything.

## Who owns a wall that sits on the boundary?

The wall on the line between two plots is the most common source of neighbour friction here, and the cause is almost always an old deed. A wall should sit inside your registered line unless the neighbour agrees in writing, and the recorded line lives in the escritura, the plano and the municipal cadastre, not in what is standing today. Water matters as much as the line: roof runoff and spouts should discharge into your own property, because sending rainwater onto a neighbour is what turns a boundary question into a claim. ## What should you check before you buy?

- **Walk the whole perimeter.** A wall that leans, bulges, or has a soft band at the base is usually taking water. A diagonal crack running out of a corner is telling you about movement.
- **Open and close the gate by hand.** A gate that drags needs new rollers or a track reset, and that is a repair rather than a service.
- **Test the motor, the remote and the intercom.** Find out where the control board and the battery live, and whether the gate still opens during a power cut.
- **Look for height added in stages.** Walls raised in pieces are often taller than the neighbour agreed to, so compare them against the plano.
- **Check the pedestrian door.** Many houses have a small street door, and it is the weak point in an otherwise solid wall: it needs a solid frame, a good lock and a reja.
- **Confirm who owns a shared wall.** If the wall is shared with a neighbour, that belongs in writing.

## What does wall and gate maintenance cost?

Budget Q2,000 to Q6,000 a year for a normal house: one repaint or lime wash of the street wall, a gate service with a new battery, a spare remote control and small masonry repairs where a plant or a spout has eaten into the render. Ironwork is the item owners forget, and it is the piece that rusts through, so [windows, doors and ironwork](/blog/windows-doors-antigua-colonial-homes/) follow the same painting cycle.
