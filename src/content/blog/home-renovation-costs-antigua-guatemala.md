Antigua Guatemala's colonial homes are stunning, but many need work. Whether you are buying a fixer-upper in the historic center or updating a house in Jocotenango, understanding renovation costs before you commit saves thousands of dollars and months of headaches.

## How Much Does Renovation Cost in Antigua Guatemala?

A full renovation of a colonial home in Antigua typically runs between $30,000 and $120,000 USD, depending on the property's condition and your finish level. A lighter cosmetic refresh (paint, floors, fixtures) might cost $10,000 to $25,000. These numbers are significantly lower than comparable work in the United States or Europe, which is one reason foreign buyers are drawn to Antigua's older properties.

Here is a rough breakdown of where your money goes:

- **Labor:** 40-50% of total cost. Skilled masons, electricians, and plumbers in Antigua earn between Q150 and Q400 per day ($20-$52 USD). Specialized restoration workers for colonial adobe and calicanto walls charge more.
- **Materials:** 30-40%. Locally made tile, adobe blocks, and hardwood are affordable. Imported materials (European fixtures, stainless steel appliances, specialty paint) can double or triple the budget.
- **Permits and fees:** 5-10%. Building permits from the municipalidad are modest, but UNESCO restrictions in the historic center add complexity and sometimes require specific materials or techniques.
- **Contingency:** Always budget 15-20% extra. Colonial homes hide surprises behind every wall, from termite damage to crumbling foundations.

## Labor Costs: What to Expect

Guatemalan construction labor is one of the biggest advantages for foreign renovators. A general contractor (maestro de obras) typically charges Q300-Q500 per day ($40-$65 USD). Here are common daily rates:

- Mason (albañil): Q150-Q300/day
- Electrician (electricista): Q200-Q400/day
- Plumber (plomero): Q200-Q350/day
- Painter (pintor): Q150-Q250/day
- Carpenter (carpintero): Q200-Q400/day
- Roofer (techador): Q200-Q350/day

For a full crew of four to six workers, expect to pay Q1,000-Q2,500 per day total ($130-$325 USD). A typical kitchen renovation takes three to five weeks. A full house renovation runs two to four months.

## Materials: Local vs. Imported

The biggest cost variable is your material choices. Local Guatemalan materials are excellent quality and very affordable:

- **Floor tile (local ceramic):** Q50-Q120 per square meter ($6-$16 USD)
- **Hardwood flooring (cypress or cedar):** Q200-Q400 per square meter ($26-$52 USD)
- **Adobe blocks:** Q8-Q15 each ($1-$2 USD)
- **Roof tile (barro):** Q15-Q25 each ($2-$3 USD)
- **Paint (local brand):** Q200-Q350 per gallon ($26-$46 USD)

Imported materials change the equation dramatically. Italian porcelain tile can cost Q400-Q800 per square meter. A European kitchen faucet might run $300-$600, compared to $40-$80 for a local equivalent. Decide early where to splurge and where local materials are perfectly fine.

## Colonial Restoration: Special Considerations

Antigua's UNESCO World Heritage status means renovations in the historic center face additional rules. The Consejo Nacional para la Protección de Antigua Guatemala oversees preservation, and you may need to:

- Use traditional calicanto mortar (lime and sand) instead of modern cement for visible walls
- Maintain original facade colors (earth tones, approved palettes)
- Preserve original architectural features like wooden balconies, cornices, and stone doorframes
- Use traditional roof tile profiles

These requirements add cost but preserve the character that makes Antigua special. Budget an extra 10-15% for colonial-compliant materials and techniques. The payoff is a home that holds its value and contributes to the city's beauty.

## Common Renovation Projects and Costs

Here are typical project budgets for a mid-range renovation in Antigua Guatemala:

- **Kitchen renovation:** $5,000-$15,000 (custom cabinets, tile counters, new appliances)
- **Bathroom renovation:** $2,500-$8,000 per bathroom
- **New roof:** $8,000-$20,000 (depends on size and structural work needed)
- **Electrical rewiring:** $3,000-$8,000 (old colonial homes often need complete rewiring)
- **Plumbing update:** $2,000-$6,000
- **Exterior paint and facade restoration:** $2,000-$6,000
- **New floor tile throughout:** $3,000-$10,000 (depends on square meters)
- **Garden and courtyard:** $1,500-$5,000

## How to Find Reliable Workers

Word of mouth is the primary way to find construction crews in Antigua. Ask your real estate agent, neighbors, or other expats who have renovated. Some tips:

- Get at least three quotes for any major project
- Ask for references and visit completed work in person
- Pay in stages, never the full amount upfront (a common arrangement is 30% to start, 40% at midpoint, 30% on completion)
- Visit the site daily if possible, or have a trusted project manager
- Put agreements in writing, even informal ones, with scope, timeline, and payment terms

## Financing Your Renovation

Guatemalan banks do not typically offer renovation loans to foreigners. Most buyers fund renovations through savings or by arranging a mortgage that includes renovation costs. Some buyers negotiate a price reduction on the property and use the savings for repairs. If you are financing through a local bank, ask whether they will include renovation costs in the appraisal.

## Final Tips

- Visit the property with a contractor before making an offer to get a realistic estimate
- Factor renovation costs into your total purchase budget from day one
- Do not skip the structural assessment, especially for homes over 50 years old
- Season matters: the dry season (November to April) is ideal for roofing and exterior work
- Keep all receipts for materials and labor for potential tax benefits
