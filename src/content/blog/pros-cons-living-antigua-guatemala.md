Antigua Guatemala regularly tops lists of the best colonial cities in Latin America for expats. The cobblestone streets, volcano views, and year-round spring climate make it easy to see why thousands of foreigners have made the move.

But living in a UNESCO World Heritage city comes with trade-offs. After working with buyers and renters in Antigua for years, we've seen what works — and what catches newcomers off guard.

Here's an honest breakdown of the pros and cons of living in Antigua Guatemala.

## The Pros: Why Expats Love Antigua

### Year-Round Spring Climate

Antigua sits at 1,500 meters (5,000 feet) elevation, giving it what many call the "eternal spring" climate. Daytime temperatures hover between 20–28°C (68–82°F) year-round. No air conditioning needed. No heating bills. Just comfortable days and cool evenings.

The dry season (November–April) brings clear skies and perfect outdoor weather. Even the rainy season (May–October) typically delivers afternoon showers that clear by evening, leaving the city fresh and green.

### Walkable Colonial City

Antigua's compact grid makes it one of the most walkable cities in Central America. Most neighborhoods are within a 15–20 minute walk of the central plaza (Parque Central). You can walk to markets, restaurants, coffee shops, gyms, and language schools without needing a car.

For trips outside the city, Uber and inDrive are widely available and affordable — a ride across town costs $3–5.

### Expat Community and Social Life

Antigua has a well-established expat community of retirees, remote workers, and entrepreneurs. You'll find weekly networking events, yoga classes, language exchanges, and volunteer opportunities. Facebook groups like "Expats in Antigua Guatemala" are active with recommendations and events.

The city also offers excellent Spanish language schools — over a dozen options ranging from intensive one-on-one immersion to part-time group classes.

### Lower Cost of Living Than North America or Europe

A couple can live comfortably in Antigua on $1,800–$2,500 per month, including rent, utilities, groceries, and dining out. A furnished one-bedroom apartment in the city center rents for $500–$800. A three-bedroom colonial home with courtyard runs $900–$1,500 per month.

Buying property is similarly reasonable compared to US or European prices — colonial homes in need of restoration start around $80,000, while move-in-ready properties range from $150,000–$350,000 depending on size and location.

### Access to Modern Healthcare

Guatemala has private hospitals in Guatemala City (45 minutes from Antigua) that meet international standards. Hospital Las Américas and Centro Médico both have English-speaking specialists and offer procedures at 40–60% less than US prices.

Many expats maintain private health insurance through companies like Seguros GES or international plans. Routine care — dental work, checkups, minor procedures — can often be handled in Antigua itself.

### Proximity to Guatemala City Airport

La Aurora International Airport (GUA) is a 45-minute drive from Antigua. It offers direct flights to Miami, Houston, Dallas, Los Angeles, Madrid, and other major hubs. Weekend trips to the beach (Pacific coast, 2 hours) or Lake Atitlán (2.5 hours) are easy from Antigua.

## The Cons: What To Know Before You Move

### Cobblestone Streets and Accessibility

That charming cobblestone from your vacation photos? You'll walk on it every day. The uneven volcanic stone streets are hard on shoes, difficult for wheeled luggage, and nearly impossible for wheelchairs or strollers in some areas. High heels are impractical.

Older colonial homes often have narrow doorways, uneven floors, and multiple stairs — accessibility is a real consideration for anyone with mobility issues.

### Unreliable Internet (Getting Better)

While Antigua has decent internet by Guatemalan standards — fiber optic is available in most of the city — outages do happen, especially during storms or construction work. Many remote workers install a backup plan: a Claro or Tigo mobile hotspot with a local SIM card.

Starlink has become increasingly popular among full-time residents who need guaranteed uptime.

### Bureaucracy and Paperwork

Guatemala's bureaucracy moves slowly. Setting up utilities, getting a residence visa, registering a vehicle, or buying property involves multiple steps, notaries, and patience. Having a trusted local contact — a lawyer, property manager, or bilingual assistant — isn't optional; it's essential.

Property purchases require a Guatemalan lawyer, a bank trust (fideicomiso) for foreigners buying in the restricted zone, and registration with the property registry. The process typically takes 2–4 months.

### Noise and Street Life

Antigua is a living city, not a museum. Expect early morning church bells, roosters at dawn, fireworks during festivals (which are frequent), and loud music from nearby bars on weekends. Construction noise is common as colonial homes are renovated.

If you're a light sleeper, look for a property on a quieter side street or one with solid interior walls — many colonial homes have thin walls between rooms.

### Rainy Season Realities

May through October brings daily afternoon downpours. Streets can flood temporarily, and humidity rises noticeably. Colonial homes with poor ventilation can develop mold on walls and furniture. A dehumidifier becomes essential.

The rain also means more mosquitoes. Dengue is present in Guatemala, so mosquito repellent and screened windows are important.

### Limited International Shopping and Delivery

You won't get Amazon Prime here. International shipping takes weeks, is expensive, and packages sometimes get held up in customs. Most expats learn to bring essentials from home visits or rely on courier services like Aeropost or CPX for US package forwarding.

Many brand-name products available in the US or Europe simply aren't available locally — or cost significantly more.

## Who Is Antigua Guatemala Best For?

Antigua works best for:
- **Remote workers** who need a reliable base and enjoy walkable city life
- **Retirees** looking for a lower-cost, warm-climate lifestyle
- **Spanish language learners** wanting immersive study
- **Couples without young children** (international schools exist but are limited)

Antigua may not suit:
- Anyone needing wheelchair accessibility
- People who require a quiet, predictable environment
- Those with serious medical conditions needing immediate specialist care
- Shoppers who rely on Amazon Prime and fast international delivery

## Final Verdict

Antigua Guatemala offers one of the best quality-of-life packages in Central America for the right person. The climate, walkability, cost of living, and community are genuinely hard to beat. The trade-offs — cobblestone streets, bureaucracy, rainy season, and limited shopping — are real but manageable with the right expectations.

The key is visiting first. Spend two weeks in Antigua during rainy season. Walk the streets. Talk to expats who've been here 2, 5, and 10 years. Rent a colonial home for a month before buying.
