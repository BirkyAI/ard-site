# Swimming Pool Costs in Antigua Guatemala Homes

A private swimming pool in Antigua Guatemala costs between $18,000 and $55,000 to build, and between $80 and $250 a month to run and maintain. Those figures move with size, site access, and the finishes you choose, but they are the real ranges builders and agents quote in 2026. This guide covers what a pool costs, whether you need a permit, what maintenance runs, how much value it adds, and what to check before buying a home that already has one.

Antigua's climate makes outdoor water attractive almost all year. The dry months from November to April bring blazing afternoons, and the rainy season still delivers plenty of warm, sticky days. In a colonial home with a walled courtyard, a pool becomes the center of daily life. In the villages and gated communities around town, pools are more common and often bigger.

## How much does it cost to build a swimming pool in Antigua Guatemala?

Total build cost depends mostly on size, site access, and finish level. The table below reflects typical 2026 quotes for the Antigua area.

| Pool type | Typical size | Build cost (USD) |
|---|---|---|
| Plunge pool or small courtyard pool | 8 to 15 m² | $18,000 to $28,000 |
| Standard family pool, concrete | 20 to 35 m² | $28,000 to $45,000 |
| Large pool with terrace and deck | 40 m²+ | $45,000 to $55,000+ |
| Above-ground or panel pool | 15 to 25 m² | $8,000 to $15,000 |

A basic poured-concrete pool with standard tile sits near the bottom of each range. Add imported finishes, a stone deck, an integrated jacuzzi, LED lighting, or a salt-water system and the price climbs quickly. In the historic center, access is the hidden cost: a mini-excavator may not fit through a colonial doorway, so digging happens by hand and that labor shows up on the invoice.

## What drives the cost up or down?

Four factors explain most of the difference between quotes:

- **Size and depth:** every extra square meter of water and deck adds excavation, steel, concrete, plaster, and tile.
- **Site access:** tight courtyards, narrow streets, and steep village lots slow the work and raise labor costs.
- **Soil and water table:** rocky ground is slow to dig, while a high water table needs dewatering and a heavier shell.
- **Equipment and finish:** pump, filter, heater, salt system, and imported tile can add $8,000 or more.

## Do you need a permit to build a swimming pool in Antigua Guatemala?

Yes. A pool is a construction work, so it needs the same approvals as any building project: uso de suelo (land-use clearance) from the municipality and, where required, a licencia de construcción. Inside the UNESCO heritage zone, the rules are stricter and the review slower, because the pool and any deck must not alter a protected structure or its patio. Most projects also need a licensed architect or engineer to file the plans.

Two practical points matter. First, confirm the water source: in the dry season, filling a large pool from a municipal line can draw scrutiny, and a well or cistern feed is often preferred or required. Second, get the permits before you break ground. Building without them risks fines and can complicate a future sale, because a buyer's attorney will find unpermitted work during due diligence.

## What does it cost to run and maintain a pool each month?

Ongoing costs in Antigua run from about $80 a month for a small pool you maintain yourself to $250 or more for a large pool with a service contract.

- **Chemicals and water:** $30 to $80 a month, higher in the dry season when evaporation forces refills.
- **Electricity:** $25 to $60 a month to run the pump a few hours a day.
- **Cleaning and service:** $40 to $120 a month if you hire someone.
- **Equipment replacement:** allow $300 to $800 a year for pumps, filters, and repairs.

Water is the cost owners underestimate. Antigua has distinct dry months, and an uncovered pool loses significant volume to evaporation. A cover, a windbreak, or a small shaded pool cuts both water and chemical use.

## Do pools add value to an Antigua Guatemala home?

A well-built pool usually helps at resale and rental, but it will not return its full cost in a normal sale. Treat it as a lifestyle upgrade that also lifts appeal, not as an investment that pays for itself on the sale price alone.

Where a pool earns its money is in short-term rentals. Properties with a private pool and outdoor space command nightly rates 20 to 30 percent above comparable homes without one, and they book more reliably in the dry season. If you plan to rent your home on Airbnb or Vrbo, a pool is one of the strongest upgrades you can make. If you are buying purely for long-term appreciation in a neighborhood where few homes have pools, the return is softer.

## What are the alternatives to a full swimming pool?

If space, budget, or water use rules out a full pool, consider a smaller option:

- **Plunge pool:** 6 to 10 m², ideal for a tight colonial courtyard, and cheap to run.
- **Lap pool:** long and narrow, good for exercise in a slim garden.
- **Integrated jacuzzi:** lower build cost, less water, and year-round use on cool evenings.
- **Shared condo or community pool:** no build or maintenance cost, common in condos and gated communities.

A plunge pool often delivers most of the lifestyle benefit at a fraction of the cost, and it fits the scale of a restored colonial patio better than a large pool would.

## What should you check before buying a home with a pool?

An existing pool is only an asset if it works. Before you commit, verify the following:

- **Structure and leaks:** cracks, dropped tiles, and a shell that loses water faster than evaporation can explain.
- **Pump, filter, and plumbing:** age, capacity, and whether parts are locally available.
- **Water source:** how the pool is filled and whether that source is reliable in the dry season.
- **Drainage and deck:** how runoff moves in heavy rain, and whether the deck slopes away from the house.
- **Permits and maintenance records:** proof the pool was built legally and a sense of what repairs are due.

Bring your own expert or a trusted agent with pool experience, because a neglected pool can cost thousands to restore.
