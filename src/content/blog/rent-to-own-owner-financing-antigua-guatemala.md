Yes, rent-to-own and owner financing both exist in Antigua Guatemala, but neither is common here. Most sales close in cash, and a seller willing to finance you, or to lease you a house with an option to buy later, is the exception rather than the rule. When it happens it is a lawyer's job: two written contracts, a real deposit, and a firm deadline. Here is how both routes work in 2026, what they cost, and where foreign buyers get hurt.

## What is rent-to-own in Guatemala?

Rent-to-own, also called a lease option or an arrendamiento con opción de compra, is two agreements tied together. First you sign a lease, so you live in the house as a tenant and pay rent. Then you sign an option to purchase, which gives you the exclusive right to buy the property at an agreed price, inside an agreed window, if you decide to go ahead.

The option is the important half. It fixes the price and the deadline now. If you walk away, you usually lose the option fee you paid at the start. If you buy, that fee and, in some deals, part of your rent is credited against the purchase price.

## Is rent-to-own common in Antigua Guatemala?

No, and that is the honest answer. The market is small and cash-heavy: Antigua draws foreign buyers who pay from home-country savings or a cash-out refinance, and sellers know it. Why carry a three-year risk when a clean cash offer arrives in six weeks? A lease option also asks the seller to be a landlord and a lender at once, which only works if they want the rent anyway or already trust you. Treat a "rent to own" listing here as an unusual opportunity, and ask the owner why.

## How does a lease option actually work?

Guatemalan law leaves the parties free to structure this, so the details live in the two contracts. Under the Civil Code (Decreto Ley 106), a well-drafted deal spells out:

- The purchase price, fixed at signing, never "market value at the time".
- The option term, usually one to three years.
- The option fee, what you pay now for the right to buy, and whether it counts toward the price.
- The monthly rent, and how much of it is credited to the purchase.
- Who pays the property tax (IUSI), insurance, and major repairs.
- What happens if you default, if the seller dies, or if the seller sells to someone else.

There is no escrow company in Guatemala, so the money you pay for the option should sit with your notary, never in a seller's personal account.

## Rent-to-own versus owner financing versus a bank mortgage

| Route | How it works | Who it suits | Main risk |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Rent-to-own (lease option) | You rent now and buy within a set window at a fixed price | A buyer who needs a year or two to save or sell elsewhere | The seller keeps the title to the end, and you can lose the option fee |
| Owner financing | You buy now, the deed transfers to you, and you pay the seller over time under a hipoteca | A buyer a bank will not lend to, with a seller who wants the payments | Higher interest, and a default can lead to foreclosure |
| Bank mortgage | A Guatemalan bank lends 60 to 70 percent of appraised value | A buyer with local income, residency, or a large deposit | 30 to 40 percent down, 8 to 12 percent interest, 60 to 90 days to close |

## What about owner financing?

Owner financing means you buy outright, but the seller acts as the bank. The notary writes the deed (escritura pública), the property is registered in your name at the Registro de la Propiedad, and you grant the seller a mortgage (hipoteca) on the balance you still owe. You then pay monthly installments over the agreed term.

This is the cleaner of the two, because the title is yours from day one. Sellers who offer it usually want a steady return, often on a shorter note of three to five years rather than a 20-year mortgage. Expect a rate above local bank rates. For the bank route, see our guide to [Guatemala mortgages for foreign buyers](/blog/guatemala-mortgage-financing-foreigners/).

## What does a rent-to-own or seller-financed deal cost?

Whatever the structure, the ordinary costs of buying still apply. Budget about 5 to 7 percent of the price for closing costs, including the 3 percent transfer tax (usually split between buyer and seller), notary and registry fees, and a recording charge of roughly 0.5 percent. The full breakdown is in our [closing process guide](/blog/guatemala-real-estate-closing-process/). An independent attorney costs about $500 to $1,500, and an inspection runs $150 to $450.

On top of that, a rent-to-own deal adds the option fee, and owner financing adds interest. Treat the option fee like a non-refundable deposit: if you do not exercise the option, it is usually gone.

## What are the risks for a foreign buyer?

- The title stays with the seller during a lease option. If the owner dies, divorces, or sells to someone else, your option can be worth little unless it was properly drafted and recorded.
- No escrow. In the United States a neutral company holds the money; in Guatemala nothing like that exists, so every dollar depends on the contract and the notary. Remote buyers get hurt here most, as we explain in [buying sight unseen](/blog/buying-property-sight-unseen-antigua-guatemala/).
- Default. Miss the rent, or miss the deadline to exercise the option, and the deal can end with nothing to show.
- Overpaying. A seller who finances you often builds the risk and interest into the price. Compare the rent-to-own price with the cash price of similar homes.

## How do you make a rent-to-own deal safe?

1. Hire your own independent attorney, never the seller's.
2. Do the same due diligence a cash buyer does: a title search, an inspection, and proof that IUSI and municipal accounts are current.
3. Fix the price and the term in writing, in a notarial deed, and ask the notary what can be recorded at the Registro de la Propiedad.
4. Keep the option fee modest, have the notary hold it, and put every promise in the contract. A verbal "we will work something out later" is worth nothing.

## Should you rent-to-own or just buy?

Most foreign buyers in Antigua buy with cash, with a cash-out refinance at home, or with developer financing on a [pre-construction project](/blog/pre-construction-homes-antigua-guatemala/). Those routes are faster and better understood than a lease option. Rent-to-own is worth considering only when the specific house is right, when the seller is motivated, and when you need a year or two to arrange the money.
