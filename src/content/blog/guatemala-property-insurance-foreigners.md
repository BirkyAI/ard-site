If you are buying property in Guatemala as a foreigner, you have probably spent months researching titles, closing costs, and residency requirements. But there is one question that often gets overlooked until the worst moment: what happens if an earthquake cracks your colonial walls, a tropical storm floods your ground floor, or a burst pipe destroys your newly renovated kitchen?

Property insurance in Guatemala works differently than in the United States, Canada, or Europe. The market is smaller, policy language is usually in Spanish, and some coverage types Americans consider standard simply do not exist here. This guide walks you through everything you need to know to protect your Antigua Guatemala investment.

## Why Insurance Matters for Foreign Homeowners in Guatemala

Guatemala sits on the intersection of three tectonic plates. Minor tremors happen weekly, and major earthquakes are a matter of when, not if. The country also has a six-month rainy season (May through October) that brings torrential downpours, landslides in hillside areas like El Hato and San Miguel Duenas, and occasional flooding in low-lying zones.

Beyond natural disasters, you also need to consider theft, fire, liability if a guest gets injured on your property, and damage from civil unrest. Without proper coverage, one event could wipe out years of investment.

## Types of Property Insurance Available in Guatemala

### Homeowner's Insurance (Seguro de Vivienda)

This is the closest equivalent to what North Americans call homeowner's insurance. It typically covers:

- Structure damage from fire, earthquake, volcanic eruption, landslide, and windstorm
- Glass breakage and plumbing failures
- Theft and burglary (with police report requirement)
- Temporary housing if your home becomes uninhabitable

Most policies are named-peril, meaning they list exactly what is covered rather than covering everything except exclusions. Read the list carefully before signing.

### Condominium Insurance

If you buy a condo or apartment in one of Antigua's new developments, the building's HOA typically carries a master policy covering the structure and common areas. However, this rarely covers your interior finishes, appliances, or personal belongings. You need a separate contents policy for everything inside your unit.

### Rental Property Insurance

If you plan to rent out your property (long-term or on Airbnb), standard homeowner policies in Guatemala often exclude or limit coverage for tenant damage and rental income loss. You need a specialized landlord policy or an endorsement that covers:

- Loss of rental income if the property becomes uninhabitable
- Tenant-caused damage beyond the security deposit
- Liability for injuries to tenants or their guests

### Contents Only Insurance

For renters or those who own the structure outright but want coverage for belongings, contents-only policies cover furniture, electronics, art, and personal valuables. These are much cheaper than full building insurance.

## Top Insurance Providers for Foreigners in Guatemala

The Guatemalan insurance market is dominated by a handful of major companies, all regulated by the Superintendencia de Bancos (SIB). The most reputable providers include:

**Seguros G&T:** The largest insurer in Guatemala, with extensive experience covering foreign-owned properties. They offer earthquake and volcanic eruption coverage as standard inclusions in many policies. Their claims process has an English-language option for Antigua's expat community.

**Seguros Universales:** A strong competitor with competitive rates for bundled home and auto policies. Known for faster claims processing in urban areas like Antigua and Guatemala City.

**Aseguradora Rural:** Popular for properties in outlying areas and the countryside. If you are buying land or a home in San Miguel Duenas, Ciudad Vieja, or the coffee-growing highlands, Rural often has better local knowledge of risk factors.

**Mapfre Guatemala:** The local arm of the Spanish global insurer. Their policies often feel more familiar to European buyers, with clear policy language and strong customer service.

## How Much Does Property Insurance Cost in Guatemala?

Insurance costs vary based on property value, location, construction materials, and coverage levels. As a rough guide for 2026:

- **Basic structure insurance:** 0.15% to 0.35% of the property value per year. A $300,000 home would cost roughly $450 to $1,050 annually.
- **Contents coverage:** $200 to $600 per year depending on the value of furnishings and electronics.
- **Earthquake endorsement:** Often adds 30% to 50% to the base premium. Given Guatemala's seismic activity, this is not optional.
- **Flood coverage:** Not always included in standard policies. Properties near rivers or in low-lying areas need a separate flood endorsement, typically $100 to $300 per year.

For comparison, a comprehensive policy on a $400,000 colonial home in Antigua's historic center with earthquake and contents coverage typically runs $800 to $1,500 per year. That is significantly cheaper than equivalent coverage in the United States.

## Common Coverage Gaps Foreigners Miss

### Earthquake Sub-limits

Some policies have a separate sub-limit for earthquake damage, often capped at 50% or 75% of the building value. If your home is worth $500,000 and the earthquake sub-limit is 60%, the maximum payout is $300,000. Ask your agent specifically about this.

### Wall and Facade Exclusions

Colonial properties in Antigua's UNESCO-protected historic center often have adobe or volcanic stone walls that are structurally fragile. Some insurers exclude or limit coverage for traditional construction materials. If you own or are buying a colonial home, confirm this before purchasing.

### Civil Unrest and Strikes

Standard policies often exclude damage from riots, strikes, and civil commotion. Given periodic protests and road blockages in Guatemala, expat property owners should consider adding this endorsement.

### Slow-Onset Damage

Water damage from a burst pipe is covered. Water damage from gradual leaks, poor maintenance, or humidity is almost never covered. Guatemala's rainy season creates chronic humidity issues that can rot wooden beams and cause mold. These are your responsibility.

## What to Do Before Buying a Policy

1. **Get a professional property valuation.** Insurance companies in Guatemala base payouts on declared value, not market value. Underinsuring your home by 20% means any claim gets reduced by 20%.

2. **Request the policy in writing in Spanish.** Even if the agent speaks English, the official contract is in Spanish. Have a bilingual lawyer or trusted translator review it before you sign.

3. **Understand the claims process.** In Guatemala, you typically need to file a police report (denuncia) for theft-related claims, and the insurer sends an adjuster within 48 hours for property damage. Keep emergency numbers saved.

4. **Bundle for discounts.** If you also own a vehicle in Guatemala, bundling home and auto insurance with the same provider typically saves 10% to 15%.

5. **Review annually.** Property values in Antigua Guatemala have risen steadily. If your home has appreciated $50,000 since you bought the policy, your coverage limits should increase accordingly.
