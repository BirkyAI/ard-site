# Water Heaters and Hot Water in Antigua Guatemala

Most homes in Antigua Guatemala have hot water, but almost none have the central boiler and tank that buyers from the United States, Canada or Europe expect. Hot water here is made point by point: a gas or electric calentador de paso mounted on the wall of each bathroom or kitchen, or a rooftop solar heater feeding the whole house. A house can have hot water in three showers and only cold water at the kitchen sink. Count the working units before you buy.

## Do homes in Antigua Guatemala have hot water?

Yes, and the supply is reliable enough that most owners stop thinking about it after the first month. What surprises buyers is the layout. Antigua sits at about 1,530 metres, so days are mild all year and hot water was never a structural priority when these houses were built. Colonial walls of adobe, bahareque and lime plaster run 40 to 80 centimetres thick, and pulling new pipe through them is slow, dusty and expensive. The market settled on a simple answer: a small heater at each point of use instead of one central system.

## The four ways homes here heat water

### Calentador de paso (tankless gas)

The most common setup in restored colonial houses and new builds. A wall-mounted unit lights when you open the tap and heats water as it flows, drawing LP gas from the same cylinder or line as the stove. Units run Q900 to Q2,500 and installation Q300 to Q800. Ventilation to an exterior wall matters. Carbon monoxide is the one real safety issue with these, and a unit sealed inside a windowless bathroom is worth fixing before anything else on the list.

### Electric tankless and electric storage boilers

Standard in apartments, condos and many long-term rentals. Draw is high, 3,500 to 7,000 watts, so the service and the breaker have to actually support it. A tired 1970s panel will trip. Guatemalan electricity is expensive, which makes electric heating the costliest way to make hot water here.

### Solar water heaters (calentadores solares)

The best fit for this climate, and the upgrade I would push any long-term buyer to price out. Antigua gets more than 300 sunny days a year, roofs are flat, and rooftop water tanks are already a local habit. A 150 to 300 litre system installed runs roughly Q5,000 to Q18,000, and most units carry an electric backup element for the cloudy weeks of the rainy season. Payback against electric heating usually lands in three to five years.

### Electric shower heads (duchas eléctricas)

The budget option: a heated shower head costing Q250 to Q600, common in inexpensive rentals and older homes. Flow is limited and the wiring must be properly grounded. Fine as a stopgap, not something to build a renovation around.

## How much does it cost to run hot water?

Running cost is where the systems separate. A gas calentador de paso is the cheapest conventional option because LP gas is relatively inexpensive in Guatemala, and a household that heats two showers daily usually spends Q150 to Q350 a month on the extra gas. Electric heating costs two to three times as much for the same hot water. A solar heater with an electric backup element sits below both, because the sun does the work for most of the year and the element only fires on grey mornings.

## What to check before you buy

- Open every tap, not just the main shower. Count how many points actually run hot.
- Test each calentador. Scale build-up and failing ignition modules are the usual breakdowns, and replacement is cheap if you know about it in advance.
- Confirm the gas setup. Cylinder or piped, where the tank sits, and whether the heater vents to open air.
- Look at the electrical panel if the home uses electric heating or a storage boiler. Adding capacity is a real cost.
- Test water pressure. Tankless units need a minimum flow to fire, so a house fed by a gravity tinaco with no pressure pump may leave you with lukewarm showers.
- Inspect any solar heater closely: vacuum tubes, the tank, the roof mounts and the backup element.
- Ask what stays with the house. A wall-mounted calentador is treated as a fixture in most sales, but a portable electric shower head can leave with the seller.

## Rooftop heaters and the heritage zone

Solar water heaters and storage tanks live on the roof, and inside the UNESCO protected centre a rooftop installation visible from the street can require approval from the conservation board. Putting panels and tanks on rear roof slopes usually avoids the question entirely, which is also the practical advice for solar panels in general.

## Renting in Antigua? Ask about hot water in writing

Short-term furnished rentals almost always have hot water in every bathroom. Long-term unfurnished rentals often do not, and a single calentador on the main shower is common. If hot water matters to you, put it in the lease rather than discovering it on the first cold morning.

## The bottom line

Hot water in Antigua Guatemala is a solved problem, just not the way most buyers expect. Point-of-use gas heaters cover the average house, solar covers the owner who plans to stay, and electric is the expensive fallback. When you tour a property, spend five minutes testing every tap and every heater. It is the fastest way to know what you are actually buying.
