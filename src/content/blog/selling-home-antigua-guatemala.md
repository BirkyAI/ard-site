If you own a property in Antigua Guatemala and are thinking about selling, you are in a strong position. The Antigua real estate market has remained active throughout 2026, with steady demand from international buyers, remote workers, and retirees. But selling in a foreign country comes with its own set of rules. Here is what you need to know to sell your home successfully.

## Step 1: Determine a Realistic Asking Price

Pricing your property correctly is the single most important step. Overpricing leads to long days on market and eventual price reductions that make buyers wonder what is wrong. Underpricing leaves money on the table.

Start by researching comparable properties that have sold recently in your area. Work with a local real estate agent who knows the Antigua market intimately. Properties in different areas of Antigua and its surrounding villages command very different prices. A colonial restoration in the historic center is valued differently than a modern home in El Hato or a pre-construction unit in Ciudad Vieja.

A good agent will provide a comparative market analysis that accounts for location, size, condition, views, and unique features. Be realistic about what your property is worth based on data, not emotion.

## Step 2: Prepare Your Property for Sale

First impressions matter. Before listing your home, invest time in preparation. Declutter every room, repair any visible damage, and give the property a deep clean. In Antigua, curb appeal is especially important. Trim back overgrown vegetation, sweep the cobblestone entrance, and make sure the facade looks inviting.

Consider professional photography. Most buyers start their search online, and high-quality photos make the difference between a click and a scroll past. If your property has volcano views, a courtyard garden, or colonial features like original tilework or a fountain, make sure these are highlighted.

For pre-construction or off-plan sales, have clear architectural renderings, floor plans, and a timeline of construction milestones ready for prospective buyers.

## Step 3: List and Market the Property

Your listing should appear on multiple channels. A local real estate agency will list on Guatemalan MLS platforms and international sites. Your agent should also use social media, email campaigns, and their buyer network to spread the word.

English and Spanish listings are both essential. Many international buyers search in English but appreciate the credibility of a Spanish listing. Include clear information about price, size, bedrooms, bathrooms, and key features. Be upfront about utilities, HOA fees, and any special considerations like parking or access.

## Step 4: Negotiate and Accept an Offer

When an offer comes in, review it carefully with your agent. In Guatemala, offers typically include a proposed price, proposed closing date, and any contingencies such as financing approval or property inspection.

The standard earnest money deposit in Guatemala is 10% of the purchase price, held in escrow until closing. Negotiations are common and expected. Be prepared to justify your asking price with the market data you gathered in step one.

Once you accept an offer, the buyer's attorney and your attorney will draft a promesa de compraventa (purchase promise agreement). This legally binding document outlines the terms and timeline.

## Step 5: The Closing Process

Closing in Guatemala takes 45 to 90 days from the accepted offer, depending on financing. The buyer's bank will appraise the property and process the mortgage application. Your attorney will prepare the escritura publica (public deed) for transfer.

Both parties sign the deed before a Guatemalan notary (who functions as a public attorney in Guatemala's legal system). The deed is then registered with the Registro General de la Propiedad (Property Registry). This registration is what legally transfers ownership.

## Step 6: Calculate Your Net Proceeds

As a seller in Guatemala, you should account for several costs:

- **Capital gains tax:** 10% on the gain (difference between purchase price and sale price)
- **Agent commission:** Typically 3-5% of the sale price
- **Attorney fees:** For deed preparation and closing representation
- **Notary fees:** For the escritura publica
- **Registry fees:** For property registration

Your agent and attorney can provide a detailed closing statement before the sale finalizes so there are no surprises.

## Common Questions About Selling in Antigua

**Do I need to be in Guatemala to sell?** No. You can grant a power of attorney (poder especial) to your attorney, who can sign on your behalf.

**How long does it typically take to sell?** Well-priced properties in desirable areas sell within 2 to 4 months. Unique or luxury properties may take longer.

**Can a foreigner sell property in Guatemala?** Yes. Foreigners have the same property rights as Guatemalan citizens and can sell freely.

**What happens if my property is in a rental contract?** The lease typically transfers to the new owner, but this can be negotiated. Discuss with your agent and attorney.
