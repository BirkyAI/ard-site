Antigua Guatemala is one of the best places in Central America to shop for handmade crafts, textiles, and artisan goods. The city and its surrounding towns have centuries of tradition in weaving, pottery, jade carving, and leatherwork. Whether you are furnishing a new home, hunting for unique gifts, or simply exploring the local culture, the traditional markets in Antigua Guatemala offer an experience that goes far beyond ordinary shopping.

## The Mercado de Artesanías: Antigua's Main Artisan Market

Located on the east side of town near the Calle del Arco area, the **Mercado de Artesanías** is the most accessible artisan market for visitors. Rows of stalls display handwoven table runners (cortes and huipiles), embroidered blouses, colorful hammocks, leather bags, and carved wooden masks.

Prices here are generally reasonable, and vendors expect some friendly negotiation. A good rule of thumb: start by offering about 60 to 70 percent of the asking price and work toward a number that feels fair to both sides. Vendors appreciate respectful bargaining, and it is part of the experience.

**What to look for:**
- Handwoven textiles made on traditional backstrap looms
- Worry dolls (muñecas quitapenas), a charming Guatemalan tradition
- Jade jewelry (Guatemala was a major jade source for the ancient Maya)
- Hand-painted ceramics and pottery
- Leather goods including bags, belts, and sandals

## The Central Plaza and Street Vendors

Every day around the **Parque Central**, local vendors spread out blankets and display their goods. You will find everything from handmade jewelry to small paintings of colonial streetscapes. These vendors change locations throughout the day, so if you do not find what you want in the morning, walk by again in the afternoon.

The plaza vendors tend to have lower prices than the formal market stalls because they have no rent overhead. This is a great place to pick up small souvenirs like keychains, magnets, and woven bracelets.

## Sunday Markets in Nearby Towns

Some of the best artisan shopping happens outside Antigua itself, in the smaller towns that surround the valley.

**San Juan del Obispo**, just a 10-minute drive from Antigua, has a small but wonderful Sunday market where local women sell homemade chocolate, fruit wines, and traditional textiles. The town is also known for its colonial church and the bishop's palace, making it a lovely half-day outing.

**Pastores** is famous throughout Guatemala for its leather boots and shoes. Artisans here hand-craft cowboy boots, riding boots, and stylish ankle boots in a dizzying range of colors and designs. A custom pair of boots typically costs between $40 and $100 USD and takes one to two weeks to make. Many expats living in Antigua Guatemala consider a trip to Pastores an essential errand.

**San Antonio Aguas Calientes**, about 15 minutes from Antigua, is one of the best places to watch weavers work on traditional backstrap looms. Women in this town produce some of the finest handwoven textiles in the region. The local cooperative has a small showroom where you can see the weaving process and purchase directly from the artisans.

## Jade and Silver Shopping

Guatemala's Motagua Valley is one of the few sources of genuine jadeite in the world, and Antigua has several reputable jade shops. The **Museo del Jade** on 5a Avenida Sur not only displays pre-Columbian jade artifacts but also has a shop selling certified jade pieces.

When buying jade, look for shops that provide certificates of authenticity. Genuine jadeite ranges from deep green to lavender to white, and prices vary widely based on color, translucency, and craftsmanship. A simple jade pendant might cost $30 to $80 USD, while intricate carved pieces can run several hundred dollars.

Silver jewelry is also popular, with many shops along the Calle del Arco and surrounding streets offering handmade silver pieces set with local stones like obsidian, amethyst, and jade.

## Tips for Market Shopping in Antigua

A few practical tips to make your market experience more enjoyable:

- **Bring cash in quetzales.** Most market vendors do not accept credit cards, and USD exchange rates at market stalls are usually unfavorable.
- **Go early in the morning.** Markets are less crowded, vendors are fresher, and you get first pick of the best goods.
- **Bring your own bag.** Guatemala has been reducing single-use plastics, and some vendors no longer provide bags.
- **Check quality carefully.** Handmade goods vary in quality. Look at stitching on textiles, check that jade pieces are smooth and well-finished, and make sure zippers and clasps work on leather goods.
- **Support local cooperatives.** When possible, buy from women's cooperatives and community organizations. Your purchase directly supports families and preserves traditional crafts.

## Bringing It Home

For expats and property owners in Antigua Guatemala, these markets are not just tourist attractions. They are where you furnish your home with locally made furniture, find unique decorative pieces for your rental property, and connect with the artisan traditions that give Antigua its distinctive character. Many long-term residents build relationships with specific artisans and commission custom pieces, from hand-carved doors to made-to-measure furniture.

Shopping at traditional markets is one of the simplest ways to support the local economy and immerse yourself in the vibrant culture that makes Antigua Guatemala such a special place to live.
