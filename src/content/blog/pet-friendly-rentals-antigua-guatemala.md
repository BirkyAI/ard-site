## Why Pet-Friendly Rentals Are Different in Antigua

Unlike in the United States or Canada, where pet-friendly apartments and houses are common and clearly advertised, the rental market in Antigua Guatemala is more informal. Many landlords do not explicitly list their pet policy. Some are open to pets but want to discuss it first. Others have strict no-pet policies due to concerns about damage to colonial-era floors, gardens, or furniture in furnished rentals.

The key is to approach the search proactively. Instead of filtering for pet-friendly listings (which rarely exist as a category), you will do better to find a property you love and then negotiate the pet terms with the landlord. ## Which Types of Properties Are Most Pet-Friendly

Not all rentals in Antigua are equally suited for pets. Here is what to look for:

**Houses with gardens or patios.** Properties with outdoor space are the easiest to get approved for pets. Landlords feel more comfortable knowing your dog has a dedicated area rather than having to be walked through shared corridors. Many colonial homes in Antigua have beautiful interior courtyards or walled gardens that work wonderfully for pets.

**Ground-floor units.** If you are looking at apartments or condos, ground-floor units with direct access to outdoor space are much more likely to be pet-friendly than upper-floor units. This is especially true for dogs that need regular outdoor access.

**Independent houses in El Hato or other suburban areas.** Neighborhoods like El Hato, Ciudad Vieja, and San Miguel Dueñas offer houses with more land and space. Properties in these areas are often more pet-friendly because they have yards, gates, and less shared infrastructure. **Furnished vs unfurnished.** Unfurnished rentals are generally easier for pet approval because there is less furniture that could be damaged. If you are looking at furnished rentals, be prepared to offer a larger security deposit or have a frank conversation about pet policies upfront.

## What Landlords Look For in Pet Owners

Understanding the landlord's perspective helps you present yourself better. Most property owners in Antigua are concerned about three things:

**Property damage.** Colonial homes have antique tile floors, wooden doors, and carefully maintained gardens. A landlord who has had a bad experience with a previous tenant's pet may be hesitant. Your job is to show you are different by offering references, a pet deposit, and a clear plan for how you will manage your pet in the home.

**Noise and neighbors.** In shared buildings or attached homes, barking dogs can create tension with neighbors. If you have a dog, be ready to explain how you manage noise. Crate training, regular exercise, and a calm temperament all help. Ground-floor units with private gardens minimize neighbor impact.

**Furniture and furnishings.** In fully furnished rentals, pets can damage sofas, curtains, and rugs. If you are looking at a furnished rental, consider whether you can bring your own pet bed or cover the furniture. Offering to replace or repair any pet damage at move-out can go a long way.
