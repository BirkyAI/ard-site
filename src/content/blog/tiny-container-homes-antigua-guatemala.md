# Tiny Homes & Container Homes in Antigua Guatemala

A tiny home in Antigua Guatemala costs roughly $25,000 to $70,000 to build, depending on whether you convert a shipping container or build a new compact house, and land is not included. A small home, or a casita behind an existing house, now gives buyers a cheaper way in and gives owners a second rental unit without buying anything new.

## What counts as a tiny home in Antigua Guatemala?

There is no legal definition of a tiny home in Guatemala. The label covers three things people are actually building here:

- **Compact single homes** under about 50 square meters (roughly 540 square feet) on their own lot, usually in the surrounding villages.
- **Guest casitas and studios** built at the back of an existing property and rented separately. These are the most common small builds inside Antigua proper.
- **Container conversions**, where a 20 or 40 foot shipping container is cut, insulated and finished into a one or two room unit.

All three share the same logic: a smaller footprint means less land, less construction, and lower property tax (IUSI), which is 0.75 percent of registered value for most residential property in Guatemala.

## How much does it cost to build a tiny home in Antigua Guatemala?

Prices below were verified against local builders and material suppliers in October 2026 and are quoted in US dollars for construction only, without land or furniture.

| Build type | Typical cost (2026) | What drives the price |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Container conversion, 20 to 30 m² | $25,000 to $45,000 | Insulation, rust treatment and window cuts, plus interior finish |
| New compact build, 30 to 50 m² | $40,000 to $70,000 | Block or steel frame, local labor, and roof quality |
| Guest casita or studio addition | $20,000 to $40,000 | Shares utility connections with the main house |
| Modular or prefab unit | $35,000 to $60,000 | Shipping and customs add 10 to 20 percent to the kit price |
| Village lot, 300 to 600 m² | $25,000 to $90,000 | Village, road access and whether services reach the lot |

A container conversion wins on price only if you insulate it properly. Antigua sits at about 1,530 meters, so nights are cool year round, and a raw steel box stays cold and noisy. Budget 15 to 20 percent of the container cost for insulation, ventilation and moisture control, and the total lands near a simple block build.

## Can you build a container home in Antigua's historic center?

Almost never, and buyers should know this before they fall in love with a photo. The historic center is UNESCO World Heritage protected and managed by the Consejo Nacional para la Protección de la Antigua Guatemala. New construction, exterior changes and modern materials are tightly restricted, and a corrugated metal facade will not pass review there.

Outside the heritage boundary, the answer changes. The ring villages and the land between them fall under normal municipal rules: you file a *uso de suelo* request to confirm the land use, then apply for a *licencia de construcción* before building. Village municipalities are used to small homes and casitas, and a clean, low one story design usually moves through without drama. Confirm the specific rules with the municipal office in the village where you plan to build, because they differ from Antigua's own.

## Where do compact homes work best near Antigua?

The best small home sites are the villages where land is still affordable and roads are paved or graded:

- **San Miguel Dueñas** has the widest range of lot sizes and the easiest commute into Antigua.
- **Ciudad Vieja** sits just south with newer developments and reliable services.
- **Jocotenango**, next to Antigua, is convenient but lots are small and land has already appreciated.
- **El Hato**, up on the hillside, offers views and cooler air, with steeper lots and more access work.

Lots outside the heritage zone also let you run a short term rental legally, which matters more than the view. See [current listings](/properties/) for the range in each village.

## Do tiny homes make good rentals in Antigua?

Yes, and the strongest version is not a standalone tiny house. It is a casita behind a property you already own. You keep one address, one utility meter and one property tax bill, then rent the guest unit nightly or monthly. A finished one bedroom casita in a central location rents for $45 to $70 per night on short term platforms, or $450 to $700 per month furnished long term. At a $30,000 build cost, that is a gross return of 15 to 25 percent before expenses, but it depends on location and on how well you run the unit.

A standalone small home in a village performs more like a normal investment: lower price per unit, a narrower pool of long term renters, and slower resale. If your goal is income, the casita model is the one to model first.

## What are the downsides of building small?

Be honest about the trade-offs before you start:

- **Financing is harder.** Guatemalan banks rarely lend against a container conversion, and a small non standard build may need to be paid in cash or against other collateral.
- **The resale market is narrow.** A tiny home appeals to a specific buyer, so plan to hold it as a rental rather than flip it.
- **Utilities and septic still cost full price.** A water connection, septic tank and electrical service cost about the same for 40 square meters as for 120, so small does not mean half the infrastructure.
- **Permits are not standardized for containers.** Some municipalities treat them as an accessory structure and some as a full building, so check before you buy.

None of these are deal breakers, but each changes the numbers, so run them before you commit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a foreigner build and own a tiny home in Guatemala?**
Yes. Foreigners can own property and build on it in Guatemala, and the same notary, title and registration process applies whether the home is 40 or 400 square meters.

**What is the cheapest way to add a rental unit in Antigua?**
A guest casita or studio addition on a property you already own is usually the cheapest and fastest, since the utilities, land and permits are partly in place.

**Do tiny homes pay less property tax?**
They can. IUSI is 0.75 percent of the registered value for most residential property, so a lower registered value means a lower annual bill.

**Is a container home legal in Antigua Guatemala?**
Outside the heritage zone, often yes, once you have the uso de suelo and the licencia de construcción. Inside the historic center, no.
